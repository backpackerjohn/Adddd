# Dubbii — Teardown

*Category: **adjacent** (initiation scaffold, not admin execution). Researched via WebSearch only (direct page fetch blocked); every quote below is verbatim from a WebSearch result excerpt with its URL. Where excerpts conflicted or a figure could not be pinned to a live page, it is marked **unverified**.*

**Attribution correction (important):** the task brief for this teardown described Dubbii as "by the How to ADHD team." That is wrong. Dubbii is by **ADHD Love** — Rich and Rox, the UK creator couple with a ~2M-follower audience — built with the Bristol agency Gravitywell (<https://www.gravitywell.co.uk/projects/dubbii/>). Jessica McCabe's "How to ADHD" (<https://howtoadhd.com/>) is a separate brand with no app connection found. Both are creator-audience ADHD brands, which is why they get conflated; downstream docs should say "ADHD Love's Dubbii."

## What it is

- **Positioning:** "the body doubling app." Follow-along, pre-recorded videos of Rich & Rox doing tasks *with* you — housework first, then self-care and life admin. Per Gravitywell's case study, the founders "asked 30,000 of their 2 million neurodivergent followers what they struggled with the most. The overwhelming response was housework" (<https://www.gravitywell.co.uk/insights/how-we-designed-an-adhd-friendly-mobile-app/>).
- **Traction claim:** "dubbii has helped over 500,000 people with household chores, self care routines, admin tasks, and more" (<https://www.dubbii.app/>). Note this is the vendor's own "helped X people" phrasing — not verified MAU/paying users. It reportedly hit #19 in the App Store at one point (<https://www.facebook.com/ADHDlovevids/posts/311576934961292/>).
- **Target user:** ADHD/neurodivergent adults frozen at task initiation — showers, dishes, laundry, "post & paperwork."
- **Platform:** iOS (<https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dubbii-the-body-doubling-app/id6450302677>) and Android (<https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gravitywell.adhdlove>). Phone-first; content is bundled so core journeys work offline ("All essential data for core user journeys are already in the mobile app upon installation" — Gravitywell case study).
- **Pricing:** search excerpts conflict — one roundup reports "about $4.99 monthly or $29.99 yearly" (<https://brightmind.club/blog/body-doubling-apps>); another excerpt says "$3.33 per month if you pay for an entire year" i.e. ~$40/yr (<https://mwm.ai/apps/dubbii-the-body-doubling-app/6450302677>). Exact current price: **unverified**; the reliable takeaway is "cheap, roughly $30–40/yr." Free tier: one full task free forever, swappable "every time the app updates," plus unlimited nudges (<https://www.dubbii.app/>). **No free trial, on principle** — their own TikTok is titled "Why we don't offer a free trial," arguing "free trials can be predatory, especially for individuals with ADHD" (<https://www.tiktok.com/@dubbii.app/video/7337390937632541984>).
- **Ratings:** ~4.8/5 App Store (<https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dubbii-the-body-doubling-app/id6450302677>); ~4.67/5 from ~1.2k Google Play ratings (<https://www.appbrain.com/app/dubbii-the-body-doubling-app/com.gravitywell.adhdlove>).

## Core loop & UX walkthrough

As documented in the app-store listings, vendor site, and Gravitywell's design case study:

1. **Pick a task** from a library (cleaning, showering, laundry; admin items listed as "paying bills, replying to a text, making a phone call, post & paperwork, and email clearance" — app-store copy via <https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dubbii-the-body-doubling-app/id6450302677> and <https://appshunter.io/ios/app/6450302677>).
2. **Press play and follow along.** A pre-recorded video of Rich or Rox does the task alongside you, "break[ing] down everyday tasks into micro-steps to reduce overwhelm" (<https://www.dubbii.app/>). The body double is **asynchronous** — "you're not joining anyone in real time, but instead there are pre-recorded videos for you to follow along with" (<https://www.shimmer.care/blog/best-body-doubling-apps>).
3. **Live layer:** daily live body-doubling sessions and a community "productive power-hour every 3 hours" (<https://www.dubbii.app/>, Google Play listing).
4. **Nudges, not alarms:** user-scheduled one-off/recurring prompts. Gravitywell: nudges are "friendly, helpful… instead of intrusive alarms," always user-controlled, and use diegetic sounds — "instead of playing an annoying beep or loud bell when it's time to do the dishes, they use the sound of splashing water in a washing-up bowl" (<https://www.gravitywell.co.uk/insights/how-we-designed-an-adhd-friendly-mobile-app/>).
5. **Shame-aware visual design:** the palette avoids "error-red" and the flows are built so users never hit a jarring error state (same case study).
6. **Onboarding/monetization flow:** try one full task free → subscribe for the full library, live sessions, progress tracking, unlimited reminders (<https://brightmind.club/blog/body-doubling-apps>).

## Retention & monetization mechanics

- **Flat cheap subscription, no % skims, no trial-to-charge trap.** The refusal to run free trials because they're "predatory… for individuals with ADHD" is a genuinely rare, principled stance — and strong trust marketing to this audience (<https://www.tiktok.com/@dubbii.app/video/7337390937632541984>).
- **Permanent free slice** (one task + unlimited nudges) that never auto-converts — closer to Molehill's own free-tier law than any other competitor torn down so far.
- **Content drops as the retention engine:** the free task is swappable "every time the app updates," which nudges reinstalls/returns on new content. Retention therefore depends on the video library growing — a *content treadmill*, the same economics as a fitness-video app.
- **Live power-hours every 3 hours** add a scheduled, social reason to return that static videos can't provide.
- **No dark patterns found.** No evidence of hard cancellation flows, retention screens, or forgetfulness-monetizing tricks in any excerpt reviewed. The paywall itself is the only recurring gripe (see below).

## Where it wins

1. **It solves initiation, the thing upstream of everything.** Cluster 1's skeptic verdict names Dubbii as the reason "initiation is completely open" is indefensible (`research/pain-points.md`). The crying-after-cleaning review is what a solved start-command looks like.
2. **Audience-first distribution.** 2M followers polled before a line of code; the app is the creator brand made tangible. CAC ≈ 0. This is the traction story behind "500,000 people."
3. **Warmth as a moat.** A known, loved, *para-social* body double (Rich & Rox) is emotionally different from Focusmate's live stranger — "a warm recorded presence sits better than a live stranger" (<https://brightmind.club/blog/body-doubling-apps>).
4. **Genuinely ADHD-native mechanics** other apps only market: no error-red, no free-trial trap, diegetic nudge sounds, offline-first content, permanent free task. Several of these independently converge on Molehill's own product law (flat pricing, no predatory conversion, shame-aware states).
5. **Cheap enough to survive the graveyard.** At ~$30–40/yr, an abandoned Dubbii costs less than one month of most ADHD subscriptions — abandonment is low-regret, which paradoxically protects the brand.

## Where it fails the ADHD user

Mapped to `research/pain-points.md` clusters:

1. **It never touches the dealing (Cluster 4).** Dubbii's "admin" content — "paying bills… post & paperwork… email clearance" — is a video of someone keeping you company *while you do 100% of the work*. The envelope still must be opened by the user; the consequence must be understood by the user; the waiver letter must be written by the user. Dubbii lowers the initiation cost of the ADHD tax; it recovers zero dollars of it. The TotallyADD insight ("open the envelope you have to deal with the contents") is exactly the boundary Dubbii cannot cross: it can get you to open the envelope, but the *dealing* is still yours.
2. **Static-content habituation risk (Cluster 2).** The product is a fixed library of pre-recorded videos. The 3rd, 30th, 300th viewing of the same 15-second "pick up your trash" clip is the alarm-tone problem in video form — novelty decays, and users already ask for longer/looping/more-varied videos. Dubbii's answer is a content treadmill (new drops, live sessions), which scales like a media company, not like software. JMIR's 70%-in-100-days abandonment curve (<https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897>) applies with full force, and no public retention data exists to rebut it (traction is stated as cumulative "helped 500,000 people," not active use).
3. **The user still self-initiates the fix (Cluster 1's own trap).** You must remember Dubbii exists, open it, and press play while frozen. Nudges help but are self-scheduled — the tool is *pull*, triggered by the very faculty that's offline. Nothing external re-triggers the loop when the user goes dark; a lapsed Dubbii user is simply gone until the next TikTok reminds them.
4. **Pull-based and event-blind.** Dubbii doesn't know a parking ticket arrived, doesn't know it doubles on day 14, and holds no deadlines in custody. Missed the nudge? Nothing escalates, nothing is watched, nothing is recovered. For admin specifically, initiation-on-demand without deadline custody leaves the highest-stakes failure mode (the silently doubling fine) fully open.
5. **The reviewers' sharpest critique is structural.** "Steps described rather than acted out… like 15 seconds" — the videos drift from *body doubling* (parallel presence) toward *instruction*, i.e., toward Goblin-Tools-style lists, which the research says were never the problem.

## What Molehill (the contest's Recoup) steals / avoids / must beat

**Steal:**

- **The no-free-trial stance and its marketing.** Dubbii proves that refusing predatory conversion is itself acquisition messaging for this audience. Molehill's law §7.5 (free tier never auto-converts, one-tap cancel) should be *said out loud* the way Dubbii says it on TikTok.
- **Diegetic, non-punitive notification design** (splashing-water nudge sounds, no error-red) — direct input for Molehill's notification-ladder and RSD-safe framing (law §7.6). Dubbii is existence proof that ADHD users reward this.
- **The warm named persona.** "Cleaning with Rox" made someone cry. Molehill's agent voice ("This envelope is mine now") should carry that same specific, warm identity — the agent as body double for the *dealing*, not just a parser.
- **Free slice that is genuinely useful forever** (their one-task-free ↔ Molehill's 5 items/mo) — never a decaying trial.
- **Micro-step decomposition of scary tasks** for the residual human 10% (Molehill's return checklists and call scripts are Dubbii-style follow-alongs in text).

**Avoid:**

- **Content-treadmill economics.** Never make retention depend on a growing media library. Molehill's "content" is per-creditor Playbooks — which compound as data (outcomes feed back in), not as videos that habituate.
- **Pull-only triggering.** Dubbii waits to be opened; Molehill's law §7.1 (automatic ingestion in month one) exists precisely so the loop restarts without the user.
- **Vendor-cumulative traction math.** "Helped 500,000 people" is downloads-flavored. Molehill's north stars (dollars/user-year, reactivations per scary-envelope event, week-4 re-capture — law §7.8) must stay outcome-denominated.
- **Accessibility as afterthought** — the unusable-with-VoiceOver review is a warning for Molehill's capture/confirm flows (active transcription must be screen-reader-clean).
- **Letting the core mechanic thin out** the way 15-second "steps described" videos thinned out body doubling: Molehill's equivalent failure would be Moves degrading into generic advice instead of pre-executed drafts.

**Must beat (the single thing):** **Dubbii gets the user to *start* the admin task; Molehill must make starting unnecessary.** The bar Dubbii sets is emotional (a warm presence that dissolves the freeze). Molehill wins only if snapping an envelope and confirming one extraction is *less* total burden than following even the friendliest 10-minute paperwork video — i.e., the AI does the dealing, the human does 30 seconds of capture-and-confirm, and the Tax Meter shows dollars Dubbii can never show. If Molehill's confirm flow ever feels like homework (risk assumption #4 in the brief), Dubbii-with-an-admin-playlist is the cheaper, warmer product.
