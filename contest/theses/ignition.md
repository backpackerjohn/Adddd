# T-Minus — Team ignition

**One-liner:** T-Minus is the AI that calls your phone at your freeze-prone moments and talks your body through the first two minutes — because a frozen person can't open an app, but almost anyone can answer a ringing phone.

## Target user
Maya, 34, product-marketing manager, diagnosed at 31 — squarely in the surge cohort (new ADHD diagnoses among women 23-49 nearly doubled 2020-2022, per Epic Research). Remote three days a week, medicated during work hours but not evenings. Her three daily freezes: 7:15am bed-to-shower paralysis, the 10:00am 'open the document' start, and the 8:30pm kitchen reset after meds wear off. She already owns the graveyard: Goblin Tools (loved, but 'it hands me a list'), Finch (abandoned week 3), Focusmate (used twice, never booked a third session because booking was itself a task). She does not need another list, another streak, or another human stranger on video. She needs the start command to come from outside her own head.

## Winning problem
- Cluster 1: Task Initiation Paralysis ("I know exactly what to do and cannot start") — specifically the slice the skeptic verdict left open: spontaneous, in-the-moment freeze rescue with no self-initiation required
- Cluster 2: Every Coping Tool Stops Working — attacked structurally as a design requirement, not a feature
- Secondary adjacency (v2): Cluster 6 Waiting Mode — the same trusted external time-guardian primitive extends to guarding an afternoon appointment

## Product concept
T-Minus inverts the direction of every initiation tool on the market. Dubbii, Focusmate, Flow Club, Goblin Tools, and every routine app share one fatal architecture: the frozen user must reach for the tool. But the freeze IS the inability to reach. As the research puts it, every existing fix "requires the frozen person to self-initiate the fix with the exact faculty that is offline." T-Minus removes the reach: at launch windows you contracted with your past (unfrozen) self, your phone rings — a real PSTN call from a saved contact — and a warm, adaptive AI voice runs a launch sequence: "Don't get up yet. Just sit up and put both feet on the floor. Tell me when they're down." It stays on the line through the first physical action — feet on floor, water running, document open — then deliberately hangs up. It is not a coach, not a therapist, not a planner. It is a two-to-five-minute bridge from list-item to body-in-motion.

A day with it: the night before, a 90-second call ("Tomorrow: out of bed by 7:40, start the deck at 10. Anything else? No? Sleep well."). At 7:40 the phone rings on the nightstand — answering is the entire ask; the agent negotiates her vertical and into the shower, and hangs up when she says the water is on. At 10:02 it calls again: "Deck open? I'll wait... okay, type the title slide while I'm here." At 9pm, unscheduled couch lock hits — the Black Hole Chair. She taps the Launch button on her lock screen (one tap, zero navigation, no chat UI) and her phone rings back within seconds: "Kitchen night. We're just carrying one plate to the sink. Take me with you."

There is no to-do list in T-Minus. No streaks, no overdue counters, no red badges. Missed calls are never mentioned. The only number that exists anywhere is a lifetime count of starts. And crucially, there is no app you must remember to open: you can forget T-Minus exists and it still calls you tomorrow. That single property is why this product's week 6 looks different from the graveyard's.

The thesis respects the skeptic's downgrade honestly. The verdict said initiation is not an empty field — Dubbii has 500k+ users, Focusmate externalizes starts for scheduled work — and named the genuinely unclaimed slice: "spontaneous, in-the-moment freeze rescue with no self-initiation required." T-Minus is purpose-built for exactly that slice, plus the scheduled freezes, with the trigger living outside the user in both cases. We position against Dubbii directly: their videos are static, identical on every play (habituation fuel), and require the frozen person to open an app, choose a video, and press play. Our call arrives, is different every time, and talks back.

## Core loop
1. EVENING CONTRACT (90 seconds, voice): T-Minus calls at a chosen wind-down time. User names tomorrow's 1-3 launch windows — not tasks, just moments the body needs to start moving ("out of bed 7:40", "start the report at 10"). No forms, ever. 2. THE CALL: at each window the phone rings (real call, saved contact, chosen ringtone). Answering is the only required act. 3. LAUNCH SEQUENCE: the agent runs a countdown protocol — second-person, imperative, micro-physical steps ("speaker on, walk to the bathroom, tell me when the water's running") — adapting live to resistance ("I can't" → "Okay. Just sit up. That's the whole job."). It stays on the line through the first physical action, confirms ignition, and hangs up within 5 minutes by design. 4. RESCUE PATH (unscheduled freezes): one tap on a lock-screen Launch button triggers a callback in seconds — for couch lock, shower avoidance, the 9pm kitchen. One tap is the entire self-initiation budget we ask of a frozen person; everything after arrives inbound. 5. MISS PROTOCOL (deterministic, promised in advance): unanswered call → one retry after 10 minutes → one no-guilt SMS ("Here when you want me. Reply GO for a call.") → silence. Never a third attempt, never a badge, never a mention of the miss on the next call. 6. WEEKLY TUNE (voice, 3 minutes): the agent reports answer-rate patterns and proposes one change — shift a window, swap voice, switch a window to SMS-first — and asks consent. The system visibly notices when it stops working and changes itself, instead of making the user confess failure.

## Why AI is essential
Three load-bearing jobs are impossible without LLMs. (1) NEGOTIATION: getting a frozen human into motion is a live, adaptive conversation — the agent must respond to "I can't," "five more minutes," "I don't know where to start," and shrink the ask in real time. A robocall or recorded video cannot do this; that category is called an alarm, and alarms are precisely what ADHD brains habituate through (the research's notification-blindness thread). (2) INFINITE SCRIPT VARIABILITY: habituation feeds on sameness. Every T-Minus call has a generated, never-repeated opening and phrasing, tuned to time of day, task, and what worked in past transcripts. Static content — Dubbii's videos, a fixed alarm tone — is structurally identical on play 40 as on play 1. (3) PER-PERSON LEARNING: transcripts reveal which framings move this specific user (humor vs drill-sergeant vs gentle; body-first vs environment-first) and feed the weekly self-tuning. Pre-LLM, the only product delivering adaptive spoken launch support was a human — an ADHD coach at $100+/hour or a patient partner, neither available at 7:15am daily nor at 9pm on the couch. Real-time voice models priced at cents per minute are what makes a daily, personal, conversational starter economically possible for $12/month. Without AI, T-Minus degrades into a scheduled robocall: it would habituate in weeks and could not negotiate — the product literally does not exist.

## Deliberately non-AI features
- The Launch Button: a lock-screen widget / iOS Shortcut that triggers a callback. Deterministic, zero AI, zero latency, zero decisions — at the freeze moment, reliability beats intelligence, and one tap is the maximum self-initiation a frozen person can be asked for.
- The Miss Protocol: hard-coded escalation ladder (one retry after 10 min, one SMS, then silence). Promised in onboarding and never varied — predictability is how we rebuild the trust in external signals that snoozed alarms destroyed.
- Quiet hours and do-not-call windows: user-set, absolute, and the AI can never override or 'decide' to call anyway. Trust primitive, not a model output.
- Auto-pause billing: if no call is answered and no button tapped for 21 days, the subscription pauses itself and billing stops — a dumb cron job, deliberately outside AI control, that is the anti-ADHD-tax core of the business model.
- The Starts counter: a plain lifetime tally of launches. No streaks, no AI judgment, no 'insights.' Arithmetic only, because any scoring of the user is shame surface area.
- In-call task timers (shower timer, 10-minute kitchen reset): simple visual/audio countdowns with no AI, running after the agent hangs up.

## Anti-habituation design
Cluster 2 says the median tool loses 70% of users within 100 days (JMIR 2024) and ADHD app death is 'three days of enthusiasm, then forget it exists' (Lunatask). T-Minus survives week 6 through five concrete mechanics. (1) STRUCTURAL IMMUNITY TO FORGETTING: the dominant abandonment mode is forgetting to open the app. T-Minus has no daily app-open; the product arrives inbound. You can forget it exists and it still calls tomorrow — abandonment requires an active act (blocking the number or replying STOP), which we detect instantly. (2) VARIABILITY AS ANTI-HABITUATION: alarm habituation is driven by identical stimuli; every call differs in opening line, phrasing, and tactic (generated per-call), and a ringing phone from a known contact carries social answer-reflex weight that a notification banner lost years ago. This is our riskiest bet and we instrument it directly. (3) DUOLINGO-STYLE IGNORE DETECTION: we adopt the published sleeping-bandit pattern (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020) as the template the skeptic verdict itself pointed to — answer rate, pickup latency, and launch confirmation are tracked per window; five consecutive misses on a window triggers self-modification ('These morning calls aren't landing. Want texts instead, or a different time, or a week off?') including graceful self-silencing with a standing re-entry path. The tool notices it is being ignored and changes itself, which the verdict confirmed no task/reminder product does. (4) ZERO SHAME SURFACE: no streaks to break, no overdue cascade (the 87-red-tasks death spiral), no history of misses visible anywhere, agent never references a failed launch. Amnesty is not a reset button — it is the absence of anything to reset. (5) EFFORT ASYMMETRY: total system maintenance is one 90-second evening call and one 3-minute weekly call, both inbound and voice-only. There is no elaborate setup to collapse under its own executive-function cost. Week 6 checkpoint in metrics: we measure answered-call rate and launch-confirmation rate at weeks 2/6/10 as the primary KPI — not DAU — and the bandit's job is to keep answered-rate flat where every competitor's open-rate curve cliffs.

## Competitors and gaps
- **Dubbii (body-doubling app by ADHD Love, 500k+ users)**: Static pre-recorded follow-along videos that are identical on every play (habituation fuel), and the frozen user must self-initiate: open the app, browse, choose a video, press play — the exact faculty that is offline. No proactive trigger, no live adaptation, no ignore-detection. <https://www.dubbii.app/>
- **Focusmate**: Externalizes the start only for tasks scheduled in advance with a human stranger on video; booking a session is itself an initiation task, sessions are 25-75 min desk-work shaped, and it is useless for the 7:15am bed freeze or 9pm couch lock. Social overhead makes it aversive for the RSD-prone. <https://www.focusmate.com/blog/adhd-body-double-productivity-accountability/>
- **Flow Club**: $29/mo group work sessions; same self-initiation and scheduling burden, oriented at knowledge-work blocks, not showers, kitchens, or getting out of bed — where the research shows the freeze actually lives. <https://www.flow.club/vs-focusmate>
- **Goblin Tools (Magic ToDo)**: Owns task breakdown and is beloved — but stops at the list. Breakdown was never Maya's problem; the bridge from item #1 to body-in-motion is out of scope for Goblin, which makes it our ideal upstream neighbor, not a rival. <https://goblin.tools/>
- **Mutra**: $7/mo peer task exchange for initiation; depends on posting tasks (self-initiation) and on human reciprocity and availability — no guarantee anyone shows up at your freeze moment, and asynchronous peers can't run a live launch sequence. <https://mutra.app/>
- **Finch / Routinery / Tiimo (routine + habit apps)**: All require remembering to open an app, all signal via notifications that habituate to invisibility, and none has in-the-moment rescue. Finch solved shame (non-punitive streaks) but not initiation — you can love your bird and still not get off the couch. <https://calmevo.com/best-habit-tracking-app-for-adhd/>

## Business model
$12/month or $96/year, flat, everything included — priced between Focusmate (~$8-12) and Flow Club ($29) with COGS of roughly 10-15 voice-minutes/day at cents per minute. The model is engineered against the ADHD tax the research documents (subscription flows that monetize forgetfulness; Monzo found ADHD money-management costs average ~£1,600/yr): (1) AUTO-PAUSE — 21 days with no answered call and no button tap pauses billing automatically and sends one notice; we structurally cannot earn zombie-subscription revenue, which converts Cluster 2's billing critique into our trust moat and marketing headline ('the subscription that cancels itself'). (2) Cancellation is replying STOP to a text — no flows, no retention screens. (3) No free-trial-to-annual-charge trap; trial ends in a pause, not a charge. (4) Monthly is the default at signup; annual is opt-in only, never pre-selected. The bet: in a market where the customer has been burned by every tool and billed for the corpse, provable billing integrity is acquisition fuel — this audience screenshots and shares it. Path to margin: voice costs fall quarterly; annual mix and a later partner/household tier ($18) lift ARPU without dark patterns.

## Month-one scope
SHIPS IN 30 DAYS (deliberately app-store-free): outbound PSTN calling agent (Twilio + a realtime voice LLM) with launch-sequence prompting for the five canonical freezes from the research — bed, shower, work-start, kitchen/tidy, leaving the house; 5-minute voice onboarding call; up to 3 daily launch windows; evening contract call; one-tap rescue via iOS Shortcut/lock-screen widget that requests a callback (plus 'call the contact yourself' as fallback); deterministic miss protocol (1 retry + 1 SMS + silence); rule-based adaptation v0 (5 misses on a window → offer change/pause via SMS); weekly starts-count SMS; auto-pause billing; Stripe + STOP-to-cancel. No native app required for MVP — a web onboarding page and a phone number is the whole product, which is why one founder plus one engineer can ship it this month. LATER (months 2-6): native iOS/Android app with widgets and Android quick-tile; bandit-based per-user script optimization on transcript outcomes; calendar-connected freeze-risk inference (call before the event Maya always freezes ahead of); silent co-presence mode (agent stays on the line quietly — body doubling without conversation); partner mode; Waiting-Mode Guardian (Cluster 6 expansion: 'I will call you at 3:10, hyperfocus freely until then'); voice persona marketplace.

## Riskiest assumptions
- People will answer repeated calls from an AI. The answer-reflex to a ringing phone may itself habituate, and some ADHD adults have phone aversion (Cluster 5's RSD/communication dread cuts against us). Mitigations: saved contact + chosen ringtone, SMS-first mode per window, and answered-rate as our week-2 kill metric. This is the assumption we test first, with 20 hand-recruited users, before writing much code.
- Conversational voice guidance actually moves bodies better than static content. Body-doubling efficacy evidence is 'mixed' per the arxiv VR study cited in the skeptic verdict; we assume live negotiation outperforms Dubbii's videos but have no proof until we measure launch-confirmation rates.
- A frozen person can execute even one tap. The verdict's own logic (the fix requires the offline faculty) applies to our rescue button; if in-the-moment taps don't happen, the scheduled inbound path must carry the product alone — which narrows us to predictable freezes.
- Carrier spam filtering and iOS telephony constraints don't strangle deliverability — Twilio numbers can get flagged; mitigations are registered branded calling and VoIP-push fallback, but this is real operational risk for a calls-are-the-product company.
- Unit economics hold if heavy users take 3 calls/day at 5 minutes each; voice-model pricing trends favor us but a $12 price point has thin margin at launch.
- Willingness to pay from a burned-by-47-apps audience: trust deficit is severe (that's the point of Cluster 2), and our auto-pause billing deliberately sacrifices revenue to earn it — the model only works if integrity actually converts to word-of-mouth acquisition.

## Closing argument
Other teams will pick richer-sounding clusters and inherit their poison pills. The ADHD-tax team (Cluster 4) needs bank integrations, biller phone calls, and financial-data trust — not shippable by a small team in 30 days, and the recovery-step agent flirts with liability. The time-blindness team (Cluster 3) walks into free, pre-installed OS competitors (Google/Apple time-to-leave) and a beloved incumbent (Llama Life) after the skeptic already downgraded the cluster to ~5-6 underserved. The communication-debt team (Cluster 5) fights every AI email startup plus platform API gatekeepers. The waiting-mode team (Cluster 6) builds on a mechanism the contradictions ledger says is unvalidated ('no research specifically examines waiting mode' — two competing causal stories). I chose the #1-ranked cluster (everyday score 10, five researchers converged) and — critically — I built on the exact sentence the adversarial skeptic wrote after tearing the cluster down: the open slice is 'spontaneous, in-the-moment freeze rescue with no self-initiation required.' T-Minus is that sentence turned into architecture: the trigger lives outside the user (inbound call), the rescue budget is one tap, and the launch sequence is a live negotiation no alarm, video, or list can perform. On Cluster 2, I don't promise better retention — I change what retention requires: every competitor needs the user to remember to open something, while T-Minus only needs them to answer a phone, and it detects and adapts when they stop (the Duolingo pattern the verdict confirmed no task tool has). It ships in 30 days with no app-store dependency — a phone number, a voice model, and a web page. And where every judge expects an ADHD app to quietly bill the forgetful, our subscription pauses itself. The other theses need the market to forgive their gaps. Mine was designed inside the skeptic's own verdict — attack it and you're attacking the research.
