# Molt — Team evergreen

**One-liner:** Molt is a five-anchor daily companion for ADHD adults that detects the moment you start tuning it out and sheds its own skin — new voice, new mechanics, new nudges — before your brain files it under 'ignore.'

## Target user
Maya, 34, diagnosed with ADHD at 31 — one of the post-2020 surge cohort of women 23-49 whose new-diagnosis rate nearly doubled from 2020 to 2022 (Epic Research). She has a full-time job, a partner, a household, and a planner graveyard: Todoist (abandoned with 87 overdue tasks), Notion (collapsed after the weekend she spent building it), Finch (the bird is fine, she just stopped opening it), two paper planners, and Structured. She does not need another task system; she needs five recurring things to actually fire every day — take meds, leave on time, start the laundry chain, deal with the mail pile, go to bed — and every tool that ever handled them went invisible within three weeks. She is the natural first customer named in the market research (Cluster 12) and the persona behind the 'hundreds of $$ on planners' App Store review.

## Winning problem
- The Meta-Problem: Every Coping Tool Stops Working (habituation + abandonment)
- Medication Management (the reminder-habituation slice only: reminder apps habituate and get abandoned)
- Time Blindness (the recurring-anchor slice only: leave-time and bedtime salience, not estimate correction)

## Product concept
Molt is not a task manager. It is a reminder organism that holds a hard-capped set of five recurring daily anchors — the meds dose, the leave-time, the laundry chain, the mail pile, the wind-down — and delivers them through nudges that are never the same twice. Every nudge is generated fresh by an LLM: different wording, different tone, different persona, different modality (text, voice note, lock-screen line, a question instead of a command). Underneath sits the real product: a habituation-detection engine. Every nudge is an instrument — Molt measures response latency, dismissal-versus-action ratio, and open-rate drift per anchor and per channel. When your response curve to a channel starts decaying, that is treated as a fire alarm, not a churn statistic: the engine mutates harder, rotates modality, or honestly retires the channel ('You've swiped the 8am ping 4 days straight. I'm going to stop pinging and try something else.'). This is Duolingo's published sleeping-bandit notification architecture (KDD 2020) — the one mainstream system proven to detect and adapt to being ignored — extended from streak reminders over a finite template bank to open-ended life logistics over a generative model, which the skeptic verdict itself confirms no to-do or reminder app currently does.

We chose the reminder/routine surface deliberately, because it proves the engine best: alarm blindness is the fastest, most legible habituation failure in the whole dataset ('invisible within weeks, sometimes days'), and reminders give the cleanest per-event telemetry — every nudge either got a response or it didn't, with a timestamp. Tasks and calendars are noisy surfaces; a nudge is a single-trial habituation experiment, dozens of times a week, per user. Molt is a machine for running those experiments and acting on them.

A day with Molt: at 7:40 a morning card arrives — today it's a three-word checklist; yesterday it was a 12-second voice note from 'Marrow,' this fortnight's persona; you one-tap confirm today's anchors in under a minute. Through the day, anchors fire as one-tap done / not-now / stop-this nudges. Anything you don't do silently expires at midnight — no red badge, no overdue counter, no streak to break, ever. On Sunday, Molt visibly molts: new skin, a persona hand-off, one mechanic retired, one new mechanic introduced, 90-second re-onboard. If you vanish for a week — and you will, we plan for it — Molt greets you with a single question ('Same five anchors, or has life moved?'), never a backlog. If you vanish for 30 days, your billing auto-pauses and Molt hibernates. The product's whole personality is built around one bet: for this user, the abandonment curve is a design outcome, not destiny — which is exactly what the research's own contradiction #6 concluded (simple, guilt-free, no-maintenance apps retain ADHD users far better).

## Core loop
1) MORNING CARD (≤60 sec): a generated brief arrives in a fresh form each day (checklist / voice note / single question / lock-screen line); user one-tap confirms or edits today's 3-5 anchors. 2) ANCHOR NUDGES: each anchor fires at its window as a freshly generated nudge — never-repeated wording, rotating tone/persona/modality — with exactly three responses: Done / Not now / Stop this. 'Not now' can take a free-text or voice reason; the LLM reschedules or reframes on the spot. 3) TELEMETRY TICK: every response or ignore updates that anchor's habituation curve (response latency drift, dismiss:action ratio). Decay past threshold triggers escalation-by-variation (modality swap, persona interjection) — never escalation-by-volume. Three straight ignores on a channel and it self-retires, out loud. 4) MIDNIGHT AMNESTY: undone anchors expire silently; no carryover, no red, no streaks anywhere in the product. 5) SUNDAY MOLT (90 sec): visible shed — new skin, persona hand-off with continuity ('Marrow briefed me on you'), one mechanic retired, one introduced, drawn from a rotation deck and biased by that user's telemetry. 6) LAPSE RE-ENTRY: absence >72h gets a one-question shame-free restart; absence >30 days pauses billing and sends one plain re-entry note.

## Why AI is essential
Non-repetition at the atomic level is the load-bearing mechanic, and it is economically impossible without generation. Duolingo's adaptive system — the proof this approach works — runs a bandit over a finite, human-written template bank and still converges on repetition; it took a dedicated team and works for exactly one domain (streak reminders). A two-person team cannot hand-write an inexhaustible, context-aware nudge library across five life domains, four personas, and multiple modalities; pre-LLM, 'novelty as a product primitive' meant a content treadmill no startup could afford, which is why nobody built this before. The LLM does four jobs no template system can: (1) generates every nudge fresh, conditioned on anchor, time, persona, recent history, and an explicit 'never resemble the last 30 nudges for this anchor' constraint; (2) converses at the moment of friction — when the user answers 'not now, my kid is sick,' it reschedules and reframes rather than re-firing; (3) writes each week's molt (persona hand-off letters, re-onboarding copy) so change feels authored, not random; (4) performs shame-free re-entry, summarizing nothing and asking one good question. Remove the AI and Molt collapses into exactly the static reminder app the research says goes invisible in days. What deliberately does NOT use AI is just as important — see the non-AI list: all safety, forgiveness, and scheduling guarantees are deterministic code, because a user rebuilding trust in reminders cannot be asked to trust a model's whims about WHETHER a nudge fires, only about HOW it reads.

## Deliberately non-AI features
- Anchor scheduling and notification delivery: plain deterministic timers/cron on-device — the guarantee that a nudge FIRES is never delegated to a model, only its content is; a hallucinated skip would destroy the exact trust the product exists to build
- Midnight TTL expiry and the no-streak, no-overdue-counter, no-red-badge invariant: hard-coded product law, not model behavior — forgiveness must be unconditional and predictable, never 'decided' by AI
- The five-anchor hard cap: a fixed constraint that keeps Molt from becoming a system to maintain (the Notion/Todoist two-week setup-collapse failure); the model is never allowed to suggest adding more structure
- One-tap Done / Not now / Stop this response surface: fixed UI, zero generation, so the cost of responding stays near zero even when the nudge itself is novel
- Habituation telemetry capture (latency, dismiss:action ratios, decay thresholds): deterministic statistics computed locally; the model consumes these signals but never computes them
- Billing auto-pause after 30 days of inactivity and one-tap in-app cancellation: hard-coded, auditable, marketed as a guarantee
- Travel/leave-time alerts defer to Google/Apple 'time to leave' rather than rebuilding them — per the skeptic's Cluster 3 verdict, free OS features already solve traffic-corrected departure; Molt only makes the leave-anchor salient

## Anti-habituation design
Eight concrete mechanics, in two layers. DETECTION LAYER (respond to decay): (1) Per-anchor, per-channel habituation curves — response latency drift and dismiss:action ratio computed on every nudge event; a statistically rising ignore-rate is the product's primary alarm. (2) Escalation-by-variation, never volume: decay triggers modality rotation (text→voice→lock-screen→widget) and persona interjection, because repetition-louder is the documented failure mode of alarms. (3) Honest self-silencing: a channel ignored 3 consecutive times retires itself and says so — the Duolingo-validated move ('these reminders don't seem to be working') that paradoxically re-earns attention. PREEMPTION LAYER (change before decay, because ADHD habituation speed is unverified — the 'days not weeks' claim is vendor-sourced, so we refuse to rely on detection alone): (4) Atomic non-repetition — no nudge for an anchor may resemble its last 30 predecessors; novelty at the message level is the default state, not a response. (5) The Sunday Molt — scheduled, visible self-change (skin, persona hand-off, one mechanic retired, one introduced) on a cadence faster than the 2-week novelty cliff and far inside the JMIR 100-day window; the app re-onboards itself in 90 seconds weekly, converting 're-onboarding' from a churn event into a ritual. (6) Midnight amnesty + no streaks: the two documented shame-abandonment triggers (87 overdue tasks; one broken streak) are structurally impossible — there is nothing in the interface that can accumulate against you. (7) Lapse-planned re-entry: >72h absence yields a one-question restart with zero backlog; the product treats abandonment as a scheduled state, not a failure, including 30-day hibernation with billing pause. (8) Week-6 responders as the only north-star metric: we instrument the JMIR cliff directly — percentage of week-1 actives still responding (not just opening) at day 42 — and every molt-deck mechanic lives or dies by its effect on that number. The 70% cliff is survived not by one trick but by making decay-detection the core competence: when mechanic X stops working for Maya, Molt is the only product in this market whose job description is noticing that and swapping X out.

## Competitors and gaps
- **Duolingo notification system (sleeping bandits, KDD 2020)**: The proof-of-concept and the ceiling of prior art: detects ignored reminders, self-silences, forgives streaks — but it is welded to one language-learning loop, draws from a finite human-written template bank, and its adaptation exists to protect Duolingo's engagement, not the user's life logistics. Nobody has ported the mechanism to daily-life anchors; the skeptic verdict explicitly found no to-do/reminder app doing Duolingo-style adaptation. <https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf>
- **Finch**: Solves shame (non-punitive streaks, beloved by ADHD users) but not habituation: the pet loop is mechanically identical every day, content is a finite library, and there is no ignore-detection — forgiveness without variation. When the bird stops being novel there is no engine underneath to notice or respond. <https://calmevo.com/best-habit-tracking-app-for-adhd/>
- **Lunatask**: Markets against the exact install-abandon-forget cycle (their copy is in the research) but ships a static ADHD to-do app — the anti-abandonment claim is positioning, not mechanics; no telemetry-driven adaptation, no self-change, no generative nudges. <https://lunatask.app/adhd>
- **Routinery / Tiimo**: Visual routine sequencers are the epitome of what habituates: fixed sequences, identical prompts daily, streak mechanics. They are the category Molt's engine is built to outlive, and they have no answer to week 6 beyond aesthetics. <https://www.tiimoapp.com>
- **Alarmy**: Attacks reflex-swiping with friction (missions to dismiss) rather than novelty — friction defeats habituation briefly but breeds resentment and uninstall, and it cannot vary itself; it fights the swipe, not the tune-out. <https://affine.pro/blog/setting-effective-reminders-adhd>
- **Dubbii**: Owns initiation (Cluster 1) via follow-along body-doubling videos — a different problem — and its finite pre-recorded content library is itself exposed to the novelty cliff with no adaptive engine; not a competitor on habituation, and its 500k users are evidence this audience pays for companionship-shaped tools. <https://www.dubbii.app/>

## Business model
$7.99/month or $59/year, one tier, with three hard-coded anti-ADHD-tax guarantees marketed as loudly as the product itself: (1) Billing auto-pause — 30 days of zero activity pauses your subscription automatically and emails you plainly; we structurally cannot earn zombie-subscription revenue, which is the documented trap ('gamification novelty decays in 4-8 weeks while annual billing continues'). This inverts the industry's incentive: because lapsed users stop paying, our revenue is mechanically coupled to our anti-habituation engine actually working — we get paid only while the product stays alive in someone's day, so the week-6 metric IS the revenue metric. (2) One-tap in-app cancellation, no retention flow, no email-to-cancel. (3) No auto-converting free trial: 21 days free (past the 2-week cliff on purpose — we want to be judged at week 3, not day 3), converting to paid only on an explicit yes; silence means it lapses to free-read-only. Recurring pricing is honest here rather than extractive because the marginal cost is real and ongoing — every nudge is a fresh LLM generation (~small-model pennies/user/day) and the molt deck is continuously authored. Annual buyers get a pro-rated pause credit under guarantee (1). Target: the 15.5M diagnosed US adults, entering via the late-diagnosed-women surge cohort, in a ~$1.9B ADHD-app market (2025) growing double digits with no dominant winner.

## Month-one scope
SHIPS IN 30 DAYS (2-3 people, iOS first since notification control is the product): five-anchor setup with hard cap; deterministic local scheduling; LLM nudge generation via API with 4 authored personas and a 30-nudge non-resemblance window; Done/Not-now/Stop-this response surface with free-text 'not now' rescheduling; telemetry capture (latency, dismiss:action) with a simple decay threshold; escalation-by-variation v0 (modality rotation across text/voice/lock-screen line) and 3-strike channel self-retirement; midnight TTL expiry, no-streak invariant; Sunday Molt v0 (skin + persona hand-off + copy, from a hand-built 8-week deck); 72h re-entry flow; Stripe billing with auto-pause and one-tap cancel. Explicitly NOT in month one: Android (month 2-3); adaptive bandit tuning of nudge arms (start with rules + thresholds, add the bandit once we have event volume); calendar/email integrations; voice conversations; any expansion past 5 anchors; community molt-pack marketplace; clinical claims of any kind. The month-one build is deliberately a thin organism around a fat measurement loop — the 8-week molt deck plus telemetry is the experiment apparatus for finding which mutations move day-42 responder retention.

## Riskiest assumptions
- Content-level novelty actually delays notification habituation: users may habituate to the CHANNEL (the buzz, the app icon) regardless of how fresh the words are — reflex-swiping happens before conscious registration, i.e., before wording can matter. Mitigation is baked in (modality rotation, self-retirement, lock-screen surface changes), but this is the thesis-level bet; if week-3 telemetry shows latency decay identical across novel vs. repeated nudges, the engine premise needs rework. The supporting 'ADHD habituates in days' claim is vendor-SEO-sourced (AFFiNE) with no peer-reviewed backing — we treat it as hypothesis, not fact.
- The five-anchor cap holds: users burned by todo apps may still demand full task management, and a hard cap could read as 'toy' next to Todoist rather than as discipline. If churn interviews say 'too small,' the wedge is wrong even if the engine works.
- Scheduled self-change delights rather than alienates: forced weekly persona/skin change could erode trust ('my tool is being random at me'), and the sizable AuDHD segment often actively wants sameness. The molt must feel authored and continuous (hand-off letters, preserved anchors) — if opt-outs of the molt exceed adoption, the marquee mechanic becomes a settings toggle.
- Habituation telemetry is statistically usable at n=1: response-latency drift on ~5 nudges/day is a noisy signal; distinguishing 'tuning out' from 'busy week' within days, per user, may need more events than the surface generates. Fallback is leaning harder on the preemption layer (scheduled molts), which is unproven as retention math.
- Unit economics of per-nudge generation plus a 21-day free trial plus billing auto-pause: three deliberately revenue-hostile choices stack; if week-6 responder retention lands under ~25-30%, auto-pause turns the base into confetti and LTV never covers CAC in a market where paid acquisition is bid up by VC-funded ADHD apps.
- Day-42 responder retention can beat the field enough to matter: the JMIR 70%/100-day figure is a general-population floor, not an ADHD-specific benchmark, so our headline comparison lacks a true control; we must generate our own baseline cohort (static-nudge variant) in beta to prove the engine causes retention, or the primary claim is unfalsifiable.

## Evidence citations
- Median 70% of users abandon health/lifestyle apps within 100 days (JMIR 2024 scoping review, 18 studies, n=525,824) — general-population figure, used as the retention floor Molt is built against, not as an ADHD-specific stat <https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897>
- In the Inflow (ADHD-specific app) trial, ~54% of participants dropped out before week 7 — the week-6 cliff is real even for ADHD-branded products <https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0000083>
- The ADHD app cycle: 'find a new app, spend an hour setting it up, use it enthusiastically for three days, and then forget it exists' (vendor-authored, corroborated across many independent vendors per the research) <https://lunatask.app/adhd>
- Alarm/reminder habituation claimed to be faster for ADHD brains ('invisible within weeks, sometimes days') — vendor SEO source, no peer-reviewed backing found by the skeptic; treated as a hypothesis Molt's telemetry will test, and the reason the preemption layer exists <https://affine.pro/blog/setting-effective-reminders-adhd>
- Accumulating 87 overdue tasks makes opening the app trigger shame, causing permanent abandonment — the failure Molt's midnight TTL amnesty makes structurally impossible <https://thawly.ai/reviews/todoist-for-adhd>
- 'Opened the app, saw the broken streak, closed the app, never opened it again' — the failure Molt's no-streak invariant makes structurally impossible <https://medium.com/@wardtylerd/i-tested-10-habit-trackers-in-30-days-8-broke-me-the-same-way-9803ea20b228>
- Setup-heavy systems collapse within two weeks because maintaining them costs more executive function than the tasks — the reason for Molt's five-anchor hard cap <https://www.focushack.io/reviews/todoist-vs-notion-adhd/>
- Duolingo's published notification system detects ignored reminders via bandits, adapts, and self-silences — proof the adaptive-reminder mechanism works at scale; skeptic confirmed no to-do/reminder app applies it, which is Molt's whitespace <https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf>
- 'Probably spend hundreds of $$ on planners just to use them for a few weeks' — planner-graveyard persona evidence (note: a positive review of Structured, showing this cohort re-converts when a design finally fits) <https://apps.apple.com/us/app/structured-daily-planner-todo/id1499198946>
- 15.5M US adults (6.0%) had a current ADHD diagnosis in 2023 <https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm>
- New ADHD diagnoses among women 23-49 nearly doubled 2020-2022 — the surge cohort Molt targets first <https://www.epicresearch.org/articles/number-of-adhd-patients-rising-especially-among-women/>

- Reminder-app habituation and abandonment is a named member problem of the medication cluster (54% Inflow dropout; 'How can I remember to take the medication that helps me remember things?') — the meds anchor serves this without any dosing advice <https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-medication-reminder-routines-alarms/>

## Closing argument
Every other thesis in this contest has the same hidden dependency: it must survive Cluster 2, and none of them is built from it. The initiation team must displace Dubbii and Focusmate in an established category (skeptic: underserved is really 5-6, not 9) and their rescue mechanic — whatever it is — habituates like everything else. The ADHD-tax team's recovery step (calls, cancellations, disputes) runs into integrations, fintech surface area, and liability that don't fit a one-month build. The communication-debt team needs email/Slack access and trust before delivering value, and its nudges habituate too. The waiting-mode team is building on a mechanism the research itself flags as unvalidated (contradiction #7: trust-fear vs. attentional dominance imply different products). Molt is the only entry whose core competence IS the shared kill condition: the week-6 cliff every team was ordered to answer is our primary feature, our telemetry target, and — via billing auto-pause — our revenue model, so we are the only team economically incapable of ignoring our own abandonment. We took the skeptic's narrowing seriously instead of fighting it: the whitespace is not 'nobody adapts' (Duolingo does) but 'no reminder/task product does' — so we ported the one mechanism with published, large-scale proof into the surface with the fastest habituation and the cleanest telemetry, added what Duolingo structurally cannot (generative non-repetition and self-change, impossible pre-LLM), and made the two documented shame-triggers (overdue counters, broken streaks) unrepresentable in the interface. Positioning saturation is real — a dozen apps SAY 'ADHD-friendly and forgiving' — but the skeptic found outcome saturation is zero: nobody has demonstrated retention. We win because we're the only team whose product gets stronger at exactly the week everyone else's dies, and the only one whose primary claim is falsifiable by our own day-42 dashboard.
