# Recoup — Team adhd-tax

**One-liner:** Snap a photo of the scary envelope and Recoup reads it, tells you the real dollars at stake, and does 90% of the fixing — turning the ADHD tax into a running refund.

## Target user
Maya, 34, diagnosed at 31 — the exact surge cohort (new ADHD diagnoses among women 23-49 nearly doubled 2020-2022, per Epic Research). Employed marketing manager, ~$75k, has the money to pay her bills but has not opened the mail pile on the kitchen counter in six weeks. Three zombie subscriptions, a parking ticket she knows is doubling, a $140 return riding around in her trunk past the window, and a shame loop where every fee makes the next envelope harder to open. She has already tried Todoist, a paper planner, and Rocket Money. She does not need another reminder; she needs someone to open the envelope and deal with the contents.

## Winning problem
- Cluster 4: Life Admin Avoidance & the ADHD Tax (bills, mail, subscriptions, returns, fines)
- Cluster 2 (as design constraint, not target): Every Coping Tool Stops Working — habituation + abandonment

## Product concept
Recoup is an AI admin harm-reduction service built on one behavioral insight from the research: "open the envelope you have to deal with the contents. Don't open the envelope nothing to deal with" (TotallyADD forum). The avoidance is not of the paper — it is of the dealing. So Recoup decouples them. The user's only job is to point a camera at the pile (opened or unopened; envelope windows, return addresses, and franking marks carry signal) or forward a dreaded email to their Recoup address. The AI does the dealing: identifies what each item is, extracts the deadline and the dollars at stake, flags what is safely ignorable junk — telling an avoider "7 of these 11 items require nothing from you" is itself a product moment — and converts the rest into pre-executed actions.

A day with Recoup: at 9am, one notification, never more, says "One move today: your parking ticket doubles from $85 to $170 on Friday. Tap to see the pre-filled online payment — or the pre-written hardship/contest letter if you want to fight it." Maya taps, reviews the extracted details against the photo of the actual ticket (the source image is always shown side-by-side, because a hallucinated deadline would be fatal to trust), approves, done in 90 seconds. Her Tax Meter ticks: "$85 in doubling averted. $312 recovered since March." Nothing else is asked of her today.

The deliberately targeted slice is exactly what the skeptic verdict identified as the unserved remainder: physical mail triage, returns execution, fine deadlines, and the shame/avoidance loop. Rocket Money owns bank-visible subscriptions; Recoup owns the pile — the paper and inbox dread that no Plaid integration can see. For the hardest recovery steps, Recoup drafts the thing the user cannot make themselves produce: the payment-plan request ("I filed bankruptcy at age 23, in large part because I just couldn't call and work out payment plans" — ADDitude reader), the first-time late-fee waiver letter (issuers grant these routinely; nobody with ADHD-fueled shame asks), the cancellation email, the word-for-word phone script with the number pre-dialed. Recoup never moves money and never sends anything without a human tap.

The emotional design is amnesty-first. Items are never "overdue" in the UI; they re-rank by current dollars-at-stake ("$85 → $170 Friday"), because a consequence framed as money still to be saved invites action, while a red badge framed as failure triggers the documented shame-then-never-open-again spiral. Recoup is the accountant who says "here's what we can still recover," never the parent who asks why you waited.

## Core loop
1) CAPTURE (10 seconds, whenever): user snaps photos of any mail/paper — no opening, sorting, or categorizing required — or forwards emails to their @recoup address. Capture is dumb-fast and offline-safe; AI processing happens after, never blocking the camera. 2) TRIAGE (AI, async): each item gets identity (who/what), deadline, dollars-at-stake, and an ignorability verdict; junk is dismissed with an explicit "requires nothing" stamp. 3) ONE MOVE A DAY (9am, single notification): Recoup surfaces exactly one action — the highest current dollars-at-stake item — pre-executed to ~90%: pre-filled payment page, drafted waiver letter, cancellation email, return QR code with the nearest drop-off, or a call script. 4) CONFIRM (90 seconds): user checks the extraction against the source photo shown alongside, taps approve/send. 5) METER: the Tax Meter increments with real dollars averted or recovered; the weekly summary is money, not streaks. Lapse branch: if nothing is snapped and notifications are ignored 3 days running, Recoup switches framing once, then self-silences: "I'll hold everything until you snap something." The pile waits without judgment; the next scary envelope re-triggers the loop from the outside.

## Why AI is essential
The load-bearing job is reading arbitrary documents and drafting bespoke recovery actions, and both were impossible pre-LLM. (1) Triage over unbounded document space: a parking authority notice, an HOA letter, a medical EOB, a jury summons, a final-notice utility bill, and a fake-urgent car-warranty mailer all look different, come from a long tail of millions of senders, and no integration-based product (Plaid, biller APIs) will ever cover them — which is precisely why Rocket Money stops at bank-visible subscriptions. Multimodal LLMs read a crumpled photographed letter and output sender, obligation, deadline, amount, and consequence in one pass. (2) Ignorability judgment: deciding "this requires nothing from you" is a semantic classification with real stakes; rules and OCR templates cannot do it, and it is half the emotional value. (3) Recovery drafting: the fee-waiver letter, the payment-plan email, the contest-the-fine appeal, the retention-flow-resistant cancellation message — each must be composed against the specific creditor, amount, and user situation. This is the step the skeptic confirmed the whole market leaves to the user, and it is exactly the step the bankruptcy-at-23 quote shows people cannot self-initiate. Without AI, Recoup degrades into either a scanning app (capture with no dealing) or a human-VA service (unit economics of $30+/hr). With AI, the marginal cost of "dealing" approaches zero, which is what makes a $9/month price on a money-recovery service possible at all.

## Deliberately non-AI features
- Deadline math and the fine-doubling countdown: once a date and amount are human-confirmed, all countdowns, escalation projections ($85 → $170 Friday), and the 9am scheduler are deterministic code. Dates that cost money must never pass through a generative step twice.
- The Tax Meter: a plain arithmetic ledger of dollars averted/recovered. It must be exactly and boringly correct, because it is the trust anchor and the retention hook.
- Capture flow: camera, photo queue, offline storage, email forward-in. Zero AI at capture time — the snap must never lag, fail, or wait on a model, because capture is the one behavior we ask the user to sustain.
- The one-notification-per-day cap, quiet hours, and the 3-ignores-then-silence rule: hard-coded policy, not model discretion. An LLM deciding when to nag is how you habituate users out of the product.
- Human-in-the-loop send/pay gates: nothing is ever mailed, emailed, or paid without an explicit tap, and Recoup never holds payment credentials or moves money. This is a trust and safety line, kept deliberately dumb.
- Side-by-side source verification UI: every extracted date/amount is displayed next to the original photo region it came from, so the human confirms against ground truth rather than trusting the model.

## Anti-habituation design
Cluster 2's mechanics are answered point by point. (1) Structural advantage first: Recoup is externally triggered and episodic, not internally motivated and daily. Habit trackers die because the product must generate the trigger and the reward is symbolic; here the trigger is the physical mail that keeps arriving regardless of app engagement, and the reward is variable-magnitude real money — the one reward class that does not habituate, because it is not a token. A lapsed user is re-engaged by the world (a scary envelope) rather than by a notification they have gone blind to. (2) No streaks, no overdue: items cannot become "overdue" in the UI — they re-rank by current dollars-at-stake, so the 87-red-tasks shame wall (thawly.ai's documented Todoist failure) is structurally impossible. There is nothing to break, so nothing breaks the user. (3) Pile amnesty on re-entry: opening the app after 5 silent weeks shows exactly one screen — "Welcome back. One move today, worth $170" — never a backlog. Re-onboarding is shame-free by construction, answering the JMIR abandonment cliff (median 70% within 100 days, n=525,824) with a product where returning at day 101 works as well as day 2. (4) Adaptive notifications that concede: one per day max; after 3 ignores the framing changes once (deadline-consequence vs money-recovered vs "want me to just hold everything?"), then Recoup self-silences Duolingo-style ("These aren't landing — I'll wait for your next snap") rather than training reflex-swiping. The skeptic showed Duolingo proved this works (KDD 2020 bandit paper); no admin tool has copied it. (5) Week-6 concrete story: onboarding targets a first real dollar win inside week 1 (a fine caught before doubling, a waiver letter granted, a zombie trial killed — median findable value in a six-week mail pile is nonzero by definition of the cluster). By week 6, Maya's Tax Meter reads a real number like $200-400. Abandoning a habit app costs nothing; going dark on Recoup means the next $170 doubling hits unwatched — loss aversion on real money is the retention mechanism, and it strengthens rather than decays with accumulated evidence that the meter is honest. (6) If she still lapses, the auto-pause billing clause (below) means the product harmlessly hibernates instead of becoming a resented zombie charge — which is what makes her comfortable coming back.

## Competitors and gaps
- **Rocket Money**: Covers only bank-visible subscriptions and negotiable bills; blind to physical mail, returns, fines, and the shame loop. Concierge takes 30-60% of first-year savings — a skim that is itself an ADHD tax. Recoup positions against it explicitly per the skeptic verdict: Rocket Money sees your bank feed; Recoup reads your pile. <https://www.rocketmoney.com/>
- **DoNotPay**: Closest in spirit (ticket appeals, trial protection) but FTC-sanctioned January 2025 for deceptive claims, 1.8 Trustpilot dominated by complaints about its own hard-to-cancel subscription, and never designed for the avoidance/shame mechanics. The category's trust vacuum is Recoup's opening. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoNotPay>
- **Monzo / Starling ADHD banking features**: Payment reminders, pots, and salary sorting — UK-only, account-visible bills only, and still leaves every recovery step (the call, the letter, the appeal) to the user. <https://monzo.com/help/your-needs/adhd-support>
- **Hero / Actor / ADHD planner apps**: Reminder and planner layers the skeptic explicitly classified as 'not execution.' They tell you the bill exists; nobody drafts the payment-plan letter. They also inherit the full Cluster 2 abandonment curve because their reward is symbolic. <https://tryhero.app/>
- **Dubbii / body-doubling apps**: Attack initiation generally via follow-along videos; still require the user to face the envelope contents themselves and offer no document intelligence, no dollar stakes, no recovery drafting. <https://www.dubbii.app/>
- **Expensify / generic scan-and-file OCR apps**: Capture without consequences: they file documents but extract no deadlines, compute no dollars-at-stake, judge no ignorability, and draft no recovery actions. Filing is not dealing. <https://www.expensify.com/>

## Business model
Flat $9/month (or $10 with the concierge letter-sending tier later), deliberately NOT a percentage of savings — Rocket Money's 30-60% first-year-savings skim extracts the most from the people recovering the most, which is a predator's version of alignment. Three structural anti-ADHD-tax commitments, marketed loudly because they are the brand: (1) Auto-pause billing: if you do not open Recoup for a full billing month, your subscription pauses automatically and you are not charged — the product contractually cannot become the zombie subscription it exists to kill. This is the signature trust move and, per Cluster 2's subscription-forgetfulness evidence, the strongest possible differentiator in a market that monetizes ADHD lapses. (2) One-tap cancellation in-app, zero retention flow, effective immediately with pro-rata refund on annual. (3) No auto-converting free trial; instead a free tier capped at 5 items/month that stays free forever, so trial-forgetting can never bill anyone. Unit economics: vision-LLM triage plus drafting costs cents per item at 2026 prices; a user averting one $35 late fee or one $170 fine doubling per quarter is visibly net-positive against $27/quarter, and the Tax Meter makes that ROI legible inside the product — the subscription justifies itself with a number, monthly. Break-even honesty is the retention pitch: "If Recoup hasn't saved you more than it costs, the meter will say so, and cancelling takes one tap."

## Month-one scope
SHIPS IN 30 DAYS (2-3 engineers, iOS-first app + thin backend): camera capture with photo queue; email forward-in address; multimodal LLM triage (sender, type, deadline, amount, consequence, ignorability) with confidence gating; side-by-side human confirmation UI for every date/amount; deterministic deadline engine and fine-escalation countdowns; One-Move-a-Day scheduler with the notification cap and 3-ignores-self-silence rule; recovery drafts for the top 5 action types (late-fee waiver letter, payment-plan request, subscription cancellation email, fine payment walkthrough/contest letter, return checklist with carrier drop-off lookup); Tax Meter ledger; pile-amnesty re-entry screen; auto-pause billing via standard subscription APIs. All of this is app + LLM + phone camera — no hardware, no bank credentials, no FDA anything. LATER (months 2-6): optional Plaid read-only for zombie-subscription detection (only after trust exists — bank linking on day 1 is an avoider's abandonment point); voice-AI phone calls to creditors with user listening in; automated return-label generation and pickup scheduling; USPS Informed Delivery ingestion so mail is triaged before it is even opened; shared-household mode for the partner dynamic in the research; UK localization. EXPLICITLY NEVER: moving money, holding payment credentials, medication features, credit-repair claims.

## Riskiest assumptions
- The capture step survives avoidance: the thesis bets that photographing a closed envelope is emotionally cheap enough to do while opening it is not. If the wall of awful extends to pointing a camera at the pile, the loop never starts. This is the 'fix requires the failing faculty' trap applied to us, and it is testable in week 1 with 20 users and a mail pile.
- Extraction accuracy is trust-critical and asymmetric: one hallucinated deadline that costs a user a doubled fine ends the relationship (and the word-of-mouth). The human-confirm UI mitigates but also adds friction; the bet is that confirming against a shown photo is low enough friction to survive.
- Recovery drafts convert to real dollars often enough to feed the Tax Meter: fee-waiver grant rates, payment-plan acceptance, and contest-letter success are assumed but unmeasured for this population; if the meter stays near $0 for a median user's first month, the retention story collapses.
- Flat-fee willingness to pay against 'free-feeling' competitors: Rocket Money's skim is invisible, $9/month is visible. The bet is that the ADHD community's documented rage at predatory billing converts into preference for honest pricing, not just approval of it.
- Episodic engagement is compatible with subscription economics: auto-pause billing means lapsed users generate $0 instead of zombie revenue — ethically core, financially untested. Modeled LTV depends on reactivation by scary envelopes, which is plausible but unproven.
- Privacy comfort: users must photograph financial and legal documents into an AI service. The research shows they already leave these documents rotting in public view on counters, suggesting low sensitivity-in-practice, but stated privacy preferences may differ.
- The Monzo 49%/£1,600 figures are bank-commissioned self-report; the sizing bet really rests on the objective Science Advances registry data (6x default risk by 40). If the everyday recoverable-dollar frequency is lower than the survey implies, week-1 wins get scarcer.

## Closing argument
Three arguments, in ascending order of force. (1) Best-verified ground: Cluster 4 carries the strongest skeptic verdict in the dataset (0.8 — versus 0.6 for initiation paralysis and habituation, 0.62 for time blindness) and the highest AI-leverage score (10). Uniquely, its evidence includes objective national-registry outcomes (Science Advances: 6x credit default at 40), not just self-report and TikTok virality. Teams building on initiation must displace Dubbii's 500k users in a crowded body-doubling field; time-blindness teams must beat free OS-level travel alerts; waiting-mode teams build on a phenomenon with literally zero formal research and an unvalidated mechanism. I build on the cluster the adversarial process attacked hardest and dented least — and my product occupies exactly the remainder the skeptic certified as unserved: mail triage, returns, fines, and the shame loop. (2) Only cluster with a self-funding retention story: every rival product must fight the 70%-abandonment cliff armed with symbolic rewards (streaks, calm, 'progress'), which is precisely what habituates. Recoup's reward is denominated in dollars, its trigger arrives physically in the user's mailbox whether or not they open the app, and lapsing has a visible price. Where other teams must explain how they survive week 6, my product's week-6 artifact is a number: what it has been worth. (3) The business model is the moat: this category's incumbents monetize ADHD (savings skims, hard-to-cancel subscriptions, FTC sanctions). A product whose billing auto-pauses when unused is not just ethical garnish — it is the single most credible thing you can say to this audience, it is cheap for me and ruinous for skim-dependent incumbents to copy, and it converts the community's loudest grievance into distribution. Judges should ask every team two questions: 'what does your user have to show for week 6?' and 'what happens when your user forgets you exist?' I am the only thesis whose answers are 'a dollar amount' and 'the bill stops.'
