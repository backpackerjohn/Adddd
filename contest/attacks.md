# Adversarial Attacks on the Finalists

Three skeptics (market, clinical/ethical, retention/habituation) attacked each of the top two theses, instructed to default to kill. **All six voted kill** — the crown decision therefore turned on *what survives each attack*, not on who escaped unscathed.


## market skeptic vs adhd-tax — KILL (confidence 0.7)

Four independent kill lines, each cited. (1) THE ECONOMIC ANALOG ALREADY DIED, TWICE. Cushion was the incentive-aligned consumer fee-recovery company — it onboarded 1M+ consumers, converted 200,000 PAYING customers, recovered $15M in fees, raised $21.6M, and still shut down in January 2025 because it "didn't reach the scale needed to sustain the business" (https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/30/fintech-startup-cushion-shuts-down-after-8-years-and-over-20-million-in-funding/). Recoup's thesis says consumer-aligned fee recovery at ~$9/mo works; the market just ran that experiment with 200k payers and returned "no." And the "pile" wedge specifically was run by Outbox, the mail-digitization startup at $4.99/mo, which died within a year because it "had difficulty finding a repeatable and scalable acquisition channel" (https://techcrunch.com/2014/01/21/outbox-shuts-down/) — while USPS now gives away Informed Delivery mail-preview for free. Even Panasonic-bankrolled Yohana, the human life-admin concierge, wound down its service in 2025 after a "comprehensive assessment of the business environment" (https://www.channelnews.com.au/panasonics-ai-ambitions-stumble-as-consumer-apps-hit-delays-and-closures/). Every layer of this stack — mail capture, fee recovery, life-admin execution — has a corpse with more funding than a seed startup will have. (2) THE INCUMBENT IS SHIPPING IT THIS QUARTER — FOR FREE. Gmail's Gemini-powered AI Inbox, rolling out now, automatically surfaces "bills due tomorrow" and generates suggested to-dos from email content at no charge (https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gmail/gmail-is-entering-the-gemini-era/). Most of Maya's dread — utility final notices, subscription renewals, medical bills — already arrives by email, where Google reads it natively with zero capture friction. For the physical remainder, the load-bearing AI job (photograph a letter, get sender/deadline/amount/consequence, draft a waiver or appeal) is a stock capability of the free ChatGPT and Claude apps already on her phone, with free purpose-built appeal-letter GPTs on top (https://www.counterforcehealth.org/post/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-appeal-your-denied-health-insurance-claim/), and $0-tier photo-to-deadline apps like SnapDue already in both app stores (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/snapdue-deadline-tracker/id6757809280). Recoup is a UX wrapper on commodity multimodal inference with no data moat, no integration moat, and no capability its user doesn't already own. (3) THE MOAT CLAIM IS STALE. The thesis positions against Rocket Money's "30-60% skim," but Rocket Money now offers bill negotiation to FREE users on success-fee terms and a Premium+ tier that includes Agent Services and bill negotiation with NO success fee (https://www.rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/how-much-does-rocket-money-cost, https://www.rocketmoney.com/terms) — the incumbent with 5M+ members and Rocket Companies' balance sheet is already moving into fee-free agentic execution. Meanwhile Duckbill is an operating, NBC-covered Series A doing the full "AI + humans deal with your life admin, refunds, phone calls" job today (https://getduckbill.com/, https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/duckbill-6779). The niche is not open; it is occupied at both the free end and the concierge end. (4) THE BUSINESS MODEL IS SELF-DEFEATING BY DESIGN. Recoup's own riskiest-assumptions list concedes episodic engagement, and its signature features — auto-pause billing on any lapsed month, a forever-free 5-item tier, one-tap cancel with pro-rata refund — mean it monetizes ONLY actively-engaged months of a population clinically defined by lapsing, whose median app abandonment is 70% by day 100 (the thesis's own JMIR citation). Rocket Money survives precisely on the passive-revenue mechanics Recoup forswears; Cushion took the ethical-alignment path with commission-only pricing and died with 200k payers. A $9/mo product that contractually stops billing when its ADHD user stops opening it has an LTV measured in tens of dollars against consumer-app CAC of $50-200, and the mail pile is a structurally shrinking channel (paper bill volume declines every year). "The bill stops when you forget us" is a beautiful pitch and a fatal income statement.

**Surviving version:** Not a standalone $9/mo consumer subscription. Two narrower forms survive the attack: (a) an ADHD-branded document-triage-and-recovery skill/agent distributed INSIDE an existing platform (a Claude/ChatGPT app, a Rocket Money or bank partnership, an EAP/insurer benefit) where someone else pays CAC and the aligned-billing gimmick becomes a feature, not a P&L; or (b) episodic transaction pricing — free triage, pay $5-15 per executed recovery (waiver granted, fine contested, return completed) so revenue is coupled to delivered dollars rather than to sustained engagement the target population cannot sustain. Either way the defensible asset is the recovery-playbook corpus (per-creditor waiver/appeal success data), not the wrapper app.


## clinical skeptic vs adhd-tax — KILL (confidence 0.66)

Recoup's load-bearing behavioral claim — that avoidance attaches to "dealing" while photographing the pile stays emotionally cheap — is clinically wrong after the first use, and the product's own loop is what breaks it. (1) RE-COUPLING: The Wall of Awful model (Brendan Mahan; https://www.adhdrewired.com/brendan-mahan-climbing-wall-awful/) describes avoidance as emotional charge on the task CATEGORY built from accumulated failure, not on a physical operation. The first time Maya snaps the pile and gets "$85 doubles to $170 Friday" at 9am, the camera becomes a conditioned trigger for exactly the dread the envelope carried. Snapping now IS opening. The decoupling insight is one-shot; the thesis's own riskiest-assumption #1 concedes this and offers no mechanism to survive it — and its anti-habituation story (re-triggered by scary envelopes) presumes capture stays cheap forever. (2) THE SAFETY GATE RUNS ON THE BROKEN FACULTY: The entire hallucination defense is a human verifying extracted dates/amounts against a photo region — a sustained-attention detail-comparison task, i.e., the core deficit of the target population, performed on a phone in a 90-second dopamine-poor window. Automation complacency and automation bias are robust in experts and novices alike and "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" (Parasuraman & Manzey 2010, Human Factors; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055). Users will rubber-stamp; when the model hallucinates a deadline, the user has now "confirmed" it — the error arrives with a receipt proving it was her fault. That is shame-spiral fuel with an audit trail, worse than the unopened envelope. (3) THE IGNORABILITY STAMP IS THE HARM VECTOR: "7 of 11 require nothing from you" is only a product moment if those 7 are NOT human-verified — verification of junk kills the relief. So the highest-consequence classification (jury summons, IRS notice, debt-collection validation letter misread as junk) is precisely the one with no human gate, sold to a user selected for her inability to double-check. A false "requires nothing" on a summons is a bench warrant; on a collection letter it's a default judgment. No confidence threshold fixes a long-tail document space; the thesis never addresses false-negative liability. (4) THE SHADOW SHAME LEDGER: "No overdue badges" is cosmetic. Dollars-at-stake countdowns are loss-framed threats with deadlines — the canonical freeze trigger for an RSD-prone user (Dodson; one-third of ADHD patients call RSD their most impairing symptom: https://www.additudemag.com/rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-adhd-emotional-dysregulation/). On the bad EF day, the 9am threat is ignored, Friday passes, and the app now KNOWS she lost $85 — quantified, timestamped, and mutually known. The paper pile at least offered ambiguity ("don't open, nothing to deal with"); Recoup destroys that ambiguity and then self-silences during the exact deadline window (3-ignores rule), which is harm-reduction theater: respectful quiet while the fine doubles under surveillance. The app becomes the new scary envelope, except this one keeps score. (5) THE LAST 10% IS THE WALL: The bankruptcy-at-23 quote is about being unable to ASK — rejection exposure — not unable to compose. A pre-written hardship letter still requires putting your name on a plea for mercy and receiving a possible denial; for RSD that send-tap is the phone call. "90% of the fixing" outsources everything except the step that was always the wall, then books the retention story (week-1 dollar win feeding the Tax Meter) on grant rates the thesis admits are unmeasured. (6) ONE-MOVE-A-DAY IS INCOHERENT AGAINST ITS OWN USER: a six-week pile has concurrent deadlines; either the cap breaks (overwhelm returns) or Recoup knowingly sits on a ticking utility shutoff because today's move was a subscription — an ethically untenable triage the user will eventually discover the app performed silently. (7) DATA RECKLESSNESS: Users photograph medical EOBs (revealing psychiatric meds and the ADHD dx itself), debt-collection letters, and legal notices into a cloud LLM, and the thesis's stated privacy reasoning — mail rotting on counters "suggests low sensitivity-in-practice" — treats a SYMPTOM of the disorder as consent. That is ethically disqualifying on its face. A breach yields a keyed database of unpaid debts, fines, and health data for a population whose financial distress the thesis's own citation links to elevated suicide risk (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba1551). No mention of retention limits, training-data exclusion, on-device processing, or breach duty. (8) PURE PROSTHESIS, NO SCAFFOLD: evidence-based treatment for adult ADHD (Safren's CBT, JAMA 2010; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20736471/) builds graded tolerance for aversive admin; Recoup builds dependency on a service whose auto-pause economics literally require the user to keep generating scary envelopes to reactivate. When it lapses, is wrong once, or shuts down, she is back at the pile with less practiced capacity and one more brick in the wall.

**Surviving version:** A narrower "mail triage + drafting copilot" survives: (a) capture with NO dollar-countdown notifications — gain-framed weekly digest only, no deadlines pushed as threats; (b) ignorability verdicts never applied to government/legal/collection mail — those categories always surface for human eyes, with the liability honestly scoped to junk-vs-commercial classification; (c) extraction on-device or with contractual zero-retention and explicit per-document consent, marketed as the privacy line rather than the billing line; (d) the send step redesigned for RSD — Recoup sends under its own agent-of-record identity where lawful and absorbs/relays denials neutrally ("the issuer said no this time; 40% grant on second ask"), so rejection never lands on the user's name directly; (e) deadline-critical items exempted from both the one-move cap and the self-silence rule, with an explicit escalation contact (partner/household mode) chosen at onboarding as the failover for bad-EF weeks; (f) positioned as an adjunct to human support (coach, partner, or CBT skills content) rather than a standalone fix, so lapse doesn't mean unwatched losses. This version forfeits the "running refund" hook and the week-1 dollar-win retention story, but it is the version that doesn't hurt Maya on her worst day.


## retention skeptic vs adhd-tax — KILL (confidence 0.72)

The anti-habituation design fails on a four-link chain, and every link is load-bearing. (1) CAPTURE IS THE FAILING FACULTY. The entire loop bootstraps from a sustained user behavior — pointing a camera at the dread pile — and the thesis's own evidence base says this class of two-minute act is precisely what never initiates: returns sit in the trunk for weeks when the ONLY remaining step is a drop-off (https://money.usnews.com/money/personal-finance/family-finance/articles/adhd-tax-what-it-is-and-how-to-avoid-it), and Cluster 1 documents 'email/mail/returns never started despite being two-minute tasks.' The 'avoidance is of the dealing, not the paper' insight is a single forum comment (https://totallyadd.com/forums/forums/topic/why-dont-we-open-envelopes/), not a mechanism; the wall-of-awful literature says avoidance attaches to the CATEGORY. Snapping the pile is an acknowledgment act that initiates dealing — and once the app is associated with the pile, the app becomes the scary envelope. The thesis concedes this is riskiest assumption #1 and offers no mitigation. Worse, the confirm step re-attaches what capture decoupled: side-by-side verification means Maya must read the parking ticket's dates and amounts after all — the exact avoided act, now as proofreading. (2) THE REWARD STREAM IS FRONT-LOADED, THEN NEAR-ZERO. 'Variable-magnitude real money doesn't habituate' quietly assumes the money keeps coming. It doesn't: the six-week backlog is a one-time honeymoon jackpot ($200-400, weeks 1-2 — indistinguishable from the documented ADHD novelty phase), and steady-state recoverable events for an employed, mostly-autopay $75k adult are, by the thesis's OWN break-even math, 'one $35 late fee or one $170 doubling per quarter.' One reward per ~90 days against a daily 9am notification slot is not a variable-ratio schedule; it is silence punctuated by junk-mail verdicts. From week 3 the daily notification either scrapes the barrel (trains reflex-swiping — the exact habituation mechanic from https://affine.pro/blog/setting-effective-reminders-adhd) or honors its own cap and goes quiet (product vanishes). (3) THE RE-TRIGGER STORY IS CIRCULAR AND THE DUOLINGO CITATION IS INVERTED. After 3 ignores Recoup self-silences; after a dark month billing auto-pauses; there are no streaks by design. So by week 5-6 there is zero outbound force. The claimed re-engagement — 'the next scary envelope re-triggers the loop from the outside' — requires that, at the moment of peak dread, a user with documented working-memory dropouts remembers an app that has been silent for weeks and voluntarily initiates engagement with the dread object. But the BASELINE behavior when a scary envelope arrives is six weeks on the counter unopened — that is the founding observation of the product. If loss aversion on a real doubling fine cannot make Maya open an envelope, it cannot make her open an app; the thesis's retention mechanism is the exact force whose failure defines its market. And the cited proof that self-silencing works at scale cuts the other way: Duolingo's sleeping bandit produced a 0.5% DAU lift and 2% new-user retention improvement (https://www.kdd.org/kdd2020/accepted-papers/view/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for-optimizing-recurring-notificatio; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3394486.3403351) — marginal optimization layered ON TOP of streaks, leagues, an owl meme, and an intrinsic goal. Recoup deletes every pull mechanism on principle and keeps only the 0.5% one. (4) THE ADHD-SPECIFIC DATA PREDICTS DEATH AT ALMOST EXACTLY WEEK 6-7. The JMIR 70%/100-day figure is general-population (https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897); the ADHD-specific datapoint is worse and precisely timed: Inflow — an ADHD-built app with clinical motivation and human support — lost ~54% of users before week 7 (https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0000083), and Cluster 2's convergent anecdote is a 3-day-to-2-week cliff ('use it enthusiastically for three days, and then forget it exists' — https://lunatask.app/adhd). Recoup's week-by-week trajectory follows this curve exactly: week 1-2 backlog jackpot; week 3 empty notifications; week 4 three ignores, self-silence; weeks 5-6 no capture (never habituated), no notification (by design), no charge (auto-pause) — the app is maximally forgettable at precisely the moment it needs to be remembered. The coup de grâce is financial: auto-pause billing converts the inevitable engagement cliff into a REVENUE cliff. Applied to the median curve, ~70% of paying users stop generating revenue within ~3 months while CAC is already spent; the LTV model depends entirely on envelope-triggered reactivation, which link (3) shows is the one behavior the target population definitionally does not perform. The thesis's proudest ethical feature guarantees that habituation kills the business even faster than it kills the habit.

**Surviving version:** Recoup survives only if the human is removed from both ends of the loop and the business stops pretending to be an engagement product. (a) Ingestion becomes automatic, not behavioral: USPS Informed Delivery integration, a Recoup mail-forwarding/virtual-mailbox address, and inbox auto-rules mean documents are triaged with ZERO sustained user behavior — the app can then push a specific, honest dollar event ('your ticket doubles Friday: $85→$170') even to a user who has been dark for five weeks, because it learned about the ticket without her snapping anything. This converts the fictional 'external trigger' into a real one and makes the amnesty re-entry screen actually reachable. (b) Partner/household mode ships in month one, not month six: the documented dynamic is that a non-ADHD partner or the user's own 'good day' does the capture; design for episodic bulk capture events, not daily snapping. (c) Economics reprice for episodic reality: pay-per-recovery (flat $5-10 per executed save, never a percentage) or an annual 'pile insurance' fee, with success metrics defined as reactivations per scary-envelope event and dollars recovered per user-year — explicitly abandoning week-6 engagement as the KPI rather than claiming to beat it. In that form the JMIR curve is irrelevant rather than answered, which is the only honest way to survive it.


## market skeptic vs ignition — KILL (confidence 0.78)

Four independent kill vectors, each sufficient alone. (1) THE 'UNCLAIMED SLICE' IS ALREADY A COMMODITY SHELF, AND NOBODY ON IT HAS BROKEN OUT. Outbound-AI-call-at-your-weak-moment products already exist in numbers: Coach Call AI ("AI accountability partner with phone calls," https://coachcall.ai/), Overlord (https://www.overlord.app/, wake-up routines + enforcement), Morning Call – AI Alarm Clock (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/morning-call-ai-alarm-clock/id6654901061), WakeCall (https://wakecall.online/, "1000+ professionals" — i.e., tiny), UpVibe: AI Motivation Calls (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/upvibe-ai-motivation-calls/id6743141334), plus human-call services (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wake-up-call-service/id6741027747). The primitive — Twilio number + realtime voice LLM + cron — is a weekend build with zero moat; T-Minus's own month-one scope admits it ("a phone number, a voice model, and a web page"). A crowded shelf of near-identical products, all still micro, is the market's answer to the demand question: people try AI-that-calls-you and don't stay. The one company that got real scale on inbound wake calls, Wakie (1.5M users, 30M calls), found the novelty "became a little intense for every morning" and pivoted away from wake-up calls entirely (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakie, https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/10/wakie/) — direct evidence that the ringing-phone stimulus itself habituates, which guts the thesis's core anti-habituation bet. (2) THE CHANNEL IS THE ONE THE TARGET COHORT HAS TRAINED ITSELF NOT TO ANSWER. Maya is a 34-year-old millennial woman: 42% of Gen Z/millennials don't answer phone calls, 55% keep their phone on silent specifically to avoid calls, and 81% of millennials report anxiety before calls (https://kallyai.com/resources/phone-anxiety-statistics, https://www.bankmycell.com/blog/why-millennials-ignore-calls, https://www.newsweek.com/how-phone-anxiety-divides-generations-1898877). The two flagship freeze windows — 7:15am in bed and 9pm on the couch — are exactly when phones sit in Sleep/DND Focus modes that suppress rings unless the user pre-configures exceptions, which is itself the executive-function setup task the product swears it never asks for. The thesis's own riskiest-assumption #1 concedes this; the population data says the assumption fails for the beachhead persona specifically. (3) UNIT ECONOMICS ARE NEGATIVE ON EXACTLY THE USERS FOR WHOM IT WORKS. Real-world realtime voice costs run $0.18–0.46/min uncached and $0.05–0.10/min even with aggressive caching (https://hackernoon.com/openai-realtime-api-pricing-in-2026-real-world-data-from-4000-measured-sessions, https://callsphere.ai/blog/vw2c-openai-realtime-cost-per-minute-math-2026), plus Twilio PSTN legs, SMS, and branded-caller registration. A retained user takes 3 launch calls + evening contract ≈ 15–20 min/day → 450–600 min/month → roughly $25–60 COGS against a $12 price: negative gross margin that scales with success. The business then removes its only cushion on purpose: auto-pause billing forfeits the zombie-subscription revenue that keeps consumer subscription apps solvent, while the product's own cited JMIR 70%-abandonment-in-100-days curve (https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897) applies to answer rates too — yielding ~2–3 month LTVs at negative margin, minus niche CAC. 'The subscription that cancels itself' is an ethics headline and a bankruptcy mechanism. (4) PLATFORM/INCUMBENT COMPRESSION IN ONE QUARTER. ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks and Gemini Scheduled Actions already deliver proactive, scheduled AI touches at $20/mo the target user likely already pays (https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt, https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/geminis-new-scheduled-actions-feature-puts-catching-up-with-chatgpt-on-its-dayplanner); ChatGPT already has voice mode, so 'scheduled task fires a voice conversation' is an adjacent feature, not a product. Meanwhile Coach Call AI or Overlord can add a 'freeze rescue' script in a sprint, and Dubbii (500k users, distribution via ADHD Love's audience) can bolt on a scheduled AI call far more easily than T-Minus can bolt on 500k trusting users. What's left is a prompt wrapped around a phone number, sold at a loss, into a channel its buyers keep on silent.

**Surviving version:** A narrower, economically inverted version could survive: T-Minus as a B2B2C adherence layer sold to ADHD coaches, therapists, and telehealth programs ($50-150/mo per client, billed to the provider or bundled into coaching packages), where the human professional prescribes and configures the call windows (solving the setup and consent problem), the patient has clinically-anchored motivation to answer, price supports the real voice COGS, and distribution rides existing trust instead of paid CAC to a burned consumer audience. Consumer-side, only a premium-priced ($29+/mo), SMS-first-with-optional-voice 'morning launch' product for the single highest-conviction freeze (bed-to-shower) has a defensible wedge — and even that must prove week-6 answer rates before any platform ships scheduled voice check-ins natively.


## clinical skeptic vs ignition — KILL (confidence 0.78)

KILL CASE — the mechanism's efficacy and its harm are the same variable, the 'zero shame surface' claim is falsified by the delivery channel itself, and the data posture is reckless for a two-person team shipping in 30 days.

(1) THE EFFICACY/HARM FORK. The ringing phone works only if it carries social answer-reflex weight. But the user KNOWS the caller is an AI. Two branches, both fatal. Branch A: declining an AI costs nothing socially, so the decline-swipe habituates exactly like the snooze button — and the target persona sits in the demographic where the answer reflex is already dead: 81% of millennials report anxiety before phone calls and 42% of Gen Z/millennials simply don't answer (https://www.bankmycell.com/blog/why-millennials-ignore-calls, https://www.newsweek.com/how-phone-anxiety-divides-generations-1898877). The thesis's own riskiest-assumption #1 concedes this; a mechanism whose author names it a week-2 kill metric is not a winning thesis, it's a pre-registered failure. Branch B: the call DOES carry social weight — then every declined call and every answered-but-still-frozen call is a witnessed interpersonal failure, which is precisely the RSD fuel this population is defined by. You cannot tune the social pressure up for efficacy without tuning up the shame. The lever is the wound.

(2) 'ZERO SHAME SURFACE' IS ARCHITECTURALLY FALSE. A real PSTN call from a saved contact leaves OS artifacts the product cannot suppress: a red missed-call badge on the Phone app, a permanent 'T-Minus — Missed Call (3)' entry in the native call log, and voicemail prompts. The miss history the thesis swears is 'visible nowhere' is in fact recorded in the most prominent app on the phone, forever, by the delivery mechanism itself. Add the weekly tune that recites answer-rate patterns aloud ('these morning calls aren't landing' = a spoken performance review of failure), and the 21-day auto-pause notice — functionally 'even your rescue tool gave up on you' — delivered at the user's lowest point, to a population with 18.6–53% comorbid depression (Katzman et al., BMC Psychiatry 2017: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28830387/). That is an abandonment signal timed to a probable depressive episode.

(3) BAD-EF-DAY FAILURE AT EVERY JOINT. The evening contract call lands in Maya's unmedicated window — her own 8:30pm kitchen freeze proves evening EF is her worst; the system's planning step requires prospection from the very self that is offline. Time blindness guarantees mis-scheduled windows: the 10:02 call arrives mid-meeting or mid-hyperfocus, becoming an aversive interruption (the Cluster-7 transition pain the product doesn't handle), and the one-swipe fix is silencing the contact — which kills the product invisibly, because 'you never open the app' also means you never notice it stopped ringing. Same invisibility applies operationally: iOS Sleep Focus/DND silences the 7:40am call by default; making it ring requires the user to configure focus-mode exceptions at onboarding — an EF setup task — and any OS update breaks it silently. And the imperative second-person protocol ('Put both feet on the floor. Tell me when they're down') is the exact demand format that deepens paralysis via autonomy threat in the substantial demand-avoidant (PDA-overlap) subset of ADHD adults, for whom avoidance is triggered by the demand itself, not the task (https://lifeskillsadvocate.com/blog/pathological-demand-avoidance-in-adults-guide/, https://www.navigatingadhd.ca/blog/adhd-and-pathological-demand-avoidance). For those users the call doesn't bridge the freeze — it is the freeze trigger.

(4) A NEW HARM THE GRAVEYARD NEVER HAD: LIVE WITNESSED FAILURE WITH NO CLINICAL FLOOR. A to-do list fails privately. T-Minus creates the scenario where Maya answers, the agent shrinks the ask twice, and she still says 'I can't' — a real-time failed social interaction, recorded to transcript. Nothing in the spec addresses what the agent does when 'I can't get out of bed' on the fourth consecutive morning is a depressive episode, or when the user says something dark on a 7am call: no screening, no crisis pathway, no escalation, no clinician in the loop — an AI performing quasi-clinical behavioral activation, built by 'one founder plus one engineer' in a month.

(5) DEPENDENCY BY DESIGN. The business model requires the ignition to remain external forever; there is no fading or internalization protocol anywhere in the spec, which inverts the goal of every evidence-based ADHD intervention (coaching/CBT builds self-cueing). If it works, self-efficacy attribution accrues to the product, and cancellation or auto-pause is an untapered removal of a prosthetic executive function.

(6) RECKLESS DATA POSTURE. The 'why AI is essential' section explicitly plans to mine call transcripts — recordings of a diagnosed person's most vulnerable moments, medication timing, bed and shower routines, when she is home alone — sensitive health data outside HIPAA, transiting unencrypted PSTN through Twilio and a third-party realtime voice vendor, retained for model tuning by a 2-person startup. Signup itself is de facto disclosure of ADHD status. This is exactly the pattern the FTC hit BetterHelp for ($7.8M; ban on sharing health data: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-ban-betterhelp-revealing-consumers-data-including-sensitive-mental-health-information-facebook), and Washington's My Health My Data Act now creates a private right of action for it. The thesis contains not one sentence on retention, consent, encryption, or transcript deletion — for a product whose core asset IS the transcript corpus.

The thesis was 'designed inside the skeptic's verdict' on market structure, but it was designed outside the clinic: the mechanism's power source is social pressure on a rejection-sensitive population, its shame-free promise is voided by the phone OS it depends on, and its worst-day behavior — the only day that matters — is either a silenced contact or a witnessed failure.

**Surviving version:** A narrower, clinically-honest version survives: an opt-in scheduled voice launch-sequence service positioned as a bridge with a built-in taper, not a permanent prosthetic. Required changes: (a) screen for phone aversion and demand-avoidance at onboarding and default those users to SMS-first with invitation-framed, choice-based scripts ('want to try feet-on-floor, or just company?') instead of imperatives; (b) drop the 'zero shame surface' claim and mitigate the OS reality — VoIP full-screen call UI instead of PSTN where possible, auto-clearing missed-call artifacts where not, no spoken recitation of miss patterns (weekly tune proposes changes without quoting failure counts), and reframe auto-pause as a neutral scheduled check-in rather than a silence-triggered notice; (c) ephemeral transcripts by default (process-and-delete, on-device or contractual zero-retention with the voice vendor), published data policy, affirmative consent for any learning-from-transcripts; (d) a crisis floor: hard-coded detection of distress language with human-resource handoff, plus a rule that N consecutive 'I can't' mornings triggers a gentle care-suggestion, not more coaxing; (e) an explicit fading protocol (agent progressively hands the countdown to the user) marketed as the goal — 'the tool that works itself out of a job' — which also converts the dependency critique into the trust story the billing model is reaching for. This version claims the same open slice (externally-triggered start support) but only for the phone-tolerant subset, with clinical advisory input, and without promising rescue on the days it structurally cannot deliver it.


## retention skeptic vs ignition — KILL (confidence 0.78)

The anti-habituation design has a fatal architectural flaw: every anti-habituation mechanism lives INSIDE the call, but habituation happens AT THE RING. (1) STIMULUS-GATING: The "infinite script variability" (mechanic #2) is only experienced after answering. The stimulus the user actually decides on is identical every time — same saved contact name, same chosen ringtone, same 2-3 predictable daily windows. That is precisely the repeated-identical-stimulus profile the thesis's own cited evidence says ADHD brains render invisible "within weeks, sometimes days" (https://affine.pro/blog/setting-effective-reminders-adhd). Declining a call is a one-swipe reflex structurally identical to alarm snoozing; an alarm is already inbound, no-app-open, contracted by your past self, and dismissible in one gesture — and it is the canonical habituation failure in their own research. T-Minus is an alarm with an LLM stapled behind the answer gate; the LLM never gets to fight habituation because the habituated user never opens the gate. (2) THE SOCIAL ANSWER-REFLEX IS BORROWED COLLATERAL THAT EVAPORATES BY DAY 3: obligation to answer a known contact exists because a human can be offended. Maya knows it's a bot, and the Miss Protocol PROMISES zero consequence for ignoring — the zero-shame design (mechanic #4) deliberately makes declining strictly cheaper than answering, every time, for a user whose disorder is failing to do the higher-effort thing. Mechanics #2 and #4 are in direct tension: they engineered the guilt-free ignore. (3) THE COHORT IS CALL-AVERSE: 80% of Americans don't answer unknown-number calls (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2020/12/14/most-americans-dont-answer-cellphone-calls-from-unknown-numbers/), and the persona — a 34-year-old millennial woman — sits in the documented telephobia cohort: 42% of Gen Z/millennials don't answer phone calls, ~60% dread accepting calls even for work (https://www.newsweek.com/how-phone-anxiety-divides-generations-1898877, https://www.bankmycell.com/blog/why-millennials-ignore-calls). A 7:15am ring demands real-time spoken performance from an unmedicated, half-asleep, RSD-prone person — the highest-cost modality at her lowest-capacity moment. The thesis itself concedes Cluster 5 "cuts against us." (4) THE DIRECT EMPIRICAL ANALOG ALREADY RAN AND FAILED: proactive automated health calls (IVR) are 30 years old. In a refill-compliance pilot, only 24% of 2,867 automated calls to 30 enrolled, consenting participants were completely answered, and participants "were not receptive to speaking to an automated voice system" (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2588437/); IVR antidepressant-adherence calls showed little impact, with compliance WORST in younger adults (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23009333/). PSTN wake-up-call services (Snoozester, since 2005 — https://snoozester.com/) have had two decades to win morning paralysis via the inbound-call architecture and stayed micro-niche; Better Wake-Up Call needs 7 snooze callbacks because people decline call-based wake-ups exactly like alarms (https://betterwakeupcall.com/how-it-works/). These are all answer-layer failures — conversational AI fixes nothing upstream of the answer. (5) THE MAINTENANCE LOOP COMPOUNDS DECAY: call relevance depends on the nightly 90-second contract, answered in the evening when the persona is unmedicated and in couch-lock hours. Missed contract → stale windows → mistimed rings → cry-wolf → lower answer rate. And the repair channel for mechanic #3's ignore-detection is SMS — the notification channel their own evidence base says is already habituated to invisibility; the self-modification offer arrives through a dead channel. The Duolingo bandit (https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf) produced single-digit engagement lifts across hundreds of millions of users with a game loop behind the tap; a seed startup with hundreds of users cannot train it, and "5 misses → offer change" is post-hoc churn detection, not prevention. (6) WEEK-6 MATH USING THEIR OWN MECHANICS: an unwanted ring is more aversive than an ignorable badge, so decay routes to active elimination — iOS Sleep Focus silences the 7:15am call by default until the alarm is dismissed, Work Focus kills the 10am call, and STOP is one text. If answer rates track the IVR analog (~25% by week 3-4), their own auto-pause mechanic (21 days without an answered call) converts the standard JMIR decay curve into formally paused billing for a large fraction of the cohort before day 42. The company's flagship trust feature operationalizes "dead by week 6" as zero revenue. The thesis says "you can forget T-Minus exists and it still calls you tomorrow" — but JMIR's 70% abandonment measures disengagement, not deletion; push notifications also arrive inbound and still went invisible. Delivery is not engagement. The week-6 curve doesn't look different from the graveyard's; it looks like the graveyard's with a phone bill.

**Surviving version:** A narrower, honest version survives: T-Minus as a time-boxed ignition intervention, not a perpetual subscription with claimed flat retention. (a) Lead with the one-tap Launch-button rescue callback — user-pulled calls dodge the answer-reflex decay entirely because the user just asked for the ring — with scheduled calls as an opt-in secondary. (b) Sell it as an 8-12 week bridge program (like a course or coaching block) priced up-front, so the JMIR decay curve is the product's designed lifespan rather than its death; auto-pause becomes graduation, not churn. (c) Add real social stakes where the bot has none: partner/accountability mode where a human is occasionally on the line or sees launch confirmations, restoring genuine answer obligation. (d) Target the call-tolerant segment first (IVR compliance was highest in older adults, not millennial women), or make SMS-first with escalation-to-call the default for the telephobic cohort.
