# Contest Judging

Panel: behavioral-science, business/investor, and LLM-engineering judges. A fourth judge (consumer-product lens) was lost to a spurious API error mid-run (logged in BUILD_LOG R3); the panel decided with three complete scorecards plus six adversarial attacks, which carried more decision weight than raw scores.

**Panel totals (3 judges × 8 criteria × 10 pts = 240 max):** {"ignition": 190, "temporal": 177, "adhd-tax": 193, "evergreen": 143, "wildcard": 181}


## Judge: behavior

Ranking by mechanism plausibility on the bad-day test: T-Minus (64) > Recoup (61) ≈ Callie (61) > Pacenote (58) > Molt (47). The decisive behavioral variable is where the trigger lives. Initiation paralysis is a failure of self-initiated action launch while stimulus-driven responding stays intact — so an inbound phone call, which recruits an overlearned social answer-reflex requiring near-zero volitional effort, is the only interaction channel in this field that plausibly fires when the user's executive system is offline. T-Minus is the purest and most disciplined implementation of that insight: tight scope, hard hang-up, deterministic miss protocol, and honest instrumentation of its own kill metric (does the answer-reflex itself habituate into a decline-reflex?). Callie shares the mechanism but spends its habituation budget faster (3-5 calls/day across four intervention types risks call fatigue) and its month-one build is heavier. Recoup has the best evidence in the dataset and the only non-habituating reinforcer (variable-magnitude real money), but its capture step is an approach behavior toward the feared stimulus — the app can become the envelope — so it's behaviorally weaker at the trigger and stronger at the reward. Pacenote's receipts-build-trust logic is sound trust-repair psychology, but it is slow-acting (weeks of receipts) against a fast novelty cliff, and its flagship 'hands your morning back' claim rides an explicitly unvalidated mechanism: if waiting mode is attentional dominance rather than alarm distrust, a guarantee gets you there on time but doesn't release the day. Molt is the most self-aware entry but bets everything on content-level novelty defeating notification habituation — yet reflex-swiping is largely pre-attentive channel habituation that occurs before wording can matter, which Molt itself lists as risk #1; a retention engine wrapped around thin first-order value (five reminders) has nothing to retain users FOR.


### ignition — total 64/80

- pain_severity: **9** — Initiation paralysis is the #1-ranked cluster (everyday score 10, 5/12 researchers converged), occurs multiple times daily (bed, work-start, evening reset), and the freeze is volitional-but-blocked — high frequency and high subjective distress per verified first-person accounts.
- evidence_strength: **8** — Built directly on the skeptic's verdict sentence, with 4/6 cluster quotes verbatim-reconfirmed, tainted sources (thawly/focushack) flagged and used only directionally, and the vendor-sourced habituation claim explicitly labeled the riskiest bet — unusually honest evidence hygiene, docked one point because cluster confidence is only 0.6.
- underserved: **7** — Dubbii (500k users), Focusmate, Flow Club, and Mutra crowd the initiation shelf and the skeptic downgraded underserved to ~5-6, but the specific slice claimed — inbound trigger with zero self-initiation, spontaneous rescue — is the exact gap the verdict certified as open, and no incumbent is call-based.
- ai_necessity: **9** — Live negotiation with a frozen human ('I can't' → shrink the ask) cannot be pre-recorded or scripted; without the LLM the product degrades into a robocall, i.e., an alarm, which is precisely the habituating artifact the cluster documents — the AI is genuinely load-bearing at the moment of intervention.
- retention: **8** — Structurally sidesteps the dominant abandonment mode (forgetting to open the app) because the product arrives inbound; deterministic miss protocol, zero shame surface, and 4.5-minute weekly maintenance are behaviorally correct — docked for the unhedged risk that the answer-reflex converts to a decline-reflex, though they instrument this as the week-2 kill metric.
- feasibility: **7** — A phone number + Twilio + realtime voice + web onboarding is honestly shippable in 30 days with no app store; docked for carrier spam-flagging risk (existential for a calls-are-the-product company) and thin margins if heavy users take 15 voice-minutes/day at $12/mo.
- willingness_to_pay: **7** — $12/mo sits credibly between Focusmate and Flow Club, and auto-pause billing plus STOP-to-cancel is genuinely non-predatory and screenshot-shareable — but the persona is burned by 47 apps and the price/margin leaves little room if trust-conversion is slower than hoped.
- wow: **9** — 'It rings your phone and talks your body off the couch — you can forget it exists and it still calls tomorrow' names the exact experience (the reach IS the freeze) that no existing tool acknowledges; this is the strongest 'finally' moment in the field.
- Strongest point: The interaction-model inversion is behaviorally exact: answering a ringing phone from a known contact is a stimulus-driven, socially conditioned response that survives executive shutdown, so the intervention arrives through the one channel a frozen brain can still operate — and the discipline of the design (5-minute cap, deterministic miss ladder, no history of failure anywhere) shows real understanding of shame-mediated abandonment.
- Fatal weakness: The entire thesis rests on the answer-reflex not habituating: if repeated AI calls train a decline-swipe (the phone-aversion and RSD threads in this population cut directly against daily scheduled calls), the product becomes a snoozed alarm with better prosody, and there is no fallback identity — SMS-first mode is a different, weaker product.

### temporal — total 58/80

- pain_severity: **8** — The time family is real and consequential — 60-90% higher missed-appointment rates, job and relationship costs, whole mornings lost to waiting mode — but waiting mode's everyday score is 7 and the pain is event-gated rather than many-times-daily.
- evidence_strength: **8** — Anchored to the two highest surviving verdicts (waiting mode and hyperfocus at 0.75), all key quotes verbatim-confirmed, small-n and vendor-sourced claims explicitly caveated — but the flagship mechanism has, by the thesis's own citation, zero formal research behind it.
- underserved: **8** — The skeptic's own words name the wedge open ('no product occupies the trusted external time-guardian wedge'; no product does thread-parking or auto focus-hole detection), with Tiimo, BugMe, Llama Life, and free Google/Apple time-to-leave alerts adjacent but none doing custody, receipts, or closed-loop calibration.
- ai_necessity: **7** — True-cost decomposition of arbitrary calendar strings and speech-to-structured thread-parking are genuine LLM work that rule-based apps cannot do, but the product's trust core — the escalation ladder and receipts — is deliberately deterministic 2015 tech, so AI is load-bearing for accuracy and copy, not for the central guarantee.
- retention: **7** — Event-driven usage with nothing to groom, no streaks, and a calibration model that makes week 6 objectively better than week 1 is the right inversion of the novelty cliff — but receipts and error models both need weeks to mature (the cold-start-vs-cliff race they name), and the custody ritual is still a pull-based act on a bad morning.
- feasibility: **7** — Calendar API + FCM + Twilio + one prompt family is a principled month-one cut, but the zero-miss delivery guarantee is genuinely hard engineering (one miss is super-linearly destructive), and Android-first collides with where the target demographic actually lives.
- willingness_to_pay: **6** — $9/mo with no-card trial and auto-pause is fair and non-predatory, but the free-alternative squeeze is the sharpest in the field — a skeptical buyer can frame the delta as 'a phone call and a story' next to free OS time-to-leave alerts, and the felt difference must land inside one trial month.
- wow: **7** — 'Stop watching the clock; I've got it' plus a 47/0 reliability receipt will make waiting-mode sufferers lean in, and the rally co-driver metaphor is memorable — but the demo moment is quieter than an AI that phones you, and the payoff (a released morning) is deferred and probabilistic.
- Strongest point: The reliability receipt is sound trust-repair psychology (distrust from past misses is countered by accumulated, visible, boring proof), and the silently self-improving error model is the only design in the contest where the product's accuracy compounds precisely as novelty decays — a genuinely correct answer to the week-6 cliff.
- Fatal weakness: The flagship value claim rides the unvalidated half of contradiction #7: if waiting mode is attentional dominance rather than alarm-distrust, a perfect guardian still leaves the appointment squatting in working memory — Maya arrives on time but her morning never comes back, and the product collapses to a premium leave-on-time notification competing with free.

### adhd-tax — total 61/80

- pain_severity: **8** — Real-dollar losses with objective registry backing (6x credit default by 40, financial distress linked to suicide risk) and a vicious shame loop make this arguably the highest-stakes cluster, docked slightly because the pain is episodic (per-envelope) rather than several-times-daily.
- evidence_strength: **9** — Built on the single strongest skeptic verdict in the dataset (0.8), the only cluster with objective national-registry outcomes rather than self-report, all six quotes reconfirmed, and the thesis itself correctly discounts the bank-commissioned Monzo figures — the best-grounded evidence base in the contest.
- underserved: **7** — The skeptic explicitly certified the remainder (physical mail triage, returns, fines, shame loop) as unserved end-to-end, with Rocket Money bank-feed-blind and DoNotPay FTC-sanctioned — but the verdict also discounted underserved to ~6-7 and Recoup must still displace Rocket Money's mindshare in the adjacent slice.
- ai_necessity: **9** — Reading an unbounded long tail of crumpled photographed documents and drafting creditor-specific waiver/payment-plan letters is genuinely impossible with rules, templates, or integrations — this is the dataset's highest AI-leverage cluster (10) and the drafting step is exactly the one the bankruptcy-at-23 quote shows users cannot self-produce.
- retention: **6** — Variable-magnitude real money is the one reward class that resists habituation, and world-triggered episodic re-engagement is a real structural advantage — but the loop's front door is an approach behavior toward the feared stimulus: for an avoider on a bad week the app itself becomes another unopened envelope, and the 9am push is still a habituatable notification.
- feasibility: **6** — Camera + multimodal triage + confirm UI + five draft types is plausible for 30 days, but extraction accuracy on deadlines is an asymmetric trust catastrophe (one hallucinated date that doubles a fine ends the relationship and the word-of-mouth), and confidence-gating the long tail of document types is harder than the scope implies.
- willingness_to_pay: **8** — The subscription justifies itself with a monthly dollar number, flat $9 against Rocket Money's 30-60% skim is genuinely aligned pricing, and auto-pause billing is maximally credible to this audience — the strongest self-evidencing ROI in the field.
- wow: **8** — 'Snap the scary envelope and it does the dealing' lands dead-center on the community's loudest named grievance (the ADHD tax), and 'the subscription that cancels itself' is instantly shareable — just short of the visceral immediacy of a phone that rings you out of a freeze.
- Strongest point: It pairs the contest's best evidence (objective registry data, 0.8 verdict) with the only reinforcer that behavioral science says does not habituate — variable, personally meaningful money — and its Tax Meter converts retention from a motivation problem into an accounting fact.
- Fatal weakness: The loop's ignition still requires the avoider to point a camera at the object of their avoidance: if the wall of awful generalizes to the capture act (and avoidance gradients routinely generalize to stimuli associated with the feared task), the pile never enters the system and every downstream strength — triage, drafts, meter — sits idle behind an unopened app.

### evergreen — total 47/80

- pain_severity: **7** — Tool death is real, universal in this population, and expensive (planner graveyards, zombie subscriptions), but it is second-order pain — the hurt is mediated through the failure of first-order anchors (meds, leaving, laundry) whose own moment-of-failure Molt addresses only via notifications.
- evidence_strength: **6** — The meta-cluster's evidence is the most vendor-tainted in the dataset (5 of 6 quotes are content marketing, the fast-ADHD-habituation claim is SEO-sourced with no peer review) — Molt flags all of this with admirable honesty, but honesty about weak evidence does not make the evidence strong, and the whole product is built on the flagged claim.
- underserved: **6** — The skeptic found positioning saturation high (a dozen apps already sell 'forgiving ADHD-friendly'), with the genuine gap narrowed to Duolingo-style ignore-detection in reminder tools — Molt targets that slice accurately, but Finch, Lunatask, Routinery, and Structured crowd the shelf it must be discovered on.
- ai_necessity: **5** — Generative non-repetition is only load-bearing if content novelty defeats notification habituation — the thesis's own admitted riskiest assumption; if reflex-swiping is pre-attentive channel habituation (as the alarm-blindness evidence suggests), the LLM is writing beautiful copy nobody reads, and the working parts (scheduling, telemetry, TTL amnesty) are deliberately non-AI.
- retention: **5** — The anti-habituation engineering is the most thorough in the contest (telemetry, self-retiring channels, amnesty, hard caps), but it wraps the thinnest first-order value — five reminders — so there is little to retain users FOR; the Sunday Molt adds weekly re-learning cost and risks alienating the sameness-preferring AuDHD segment, and the whole surface remains the notification channel this population has documented blindness to.
- feasibility: **8** — The most buildable entry: local scheduling, LLM copy generation, Stripe, no calendar/bank/telephony dependencies, cheap per-nudge COGS — a thin organism around a measurement loop is exactly a month-one-sized build.
- willingness_to_pay: **5** — $7.99 for five smarter reminders competes with free OS alarms and free-tier habit apps; the deliberately revenue-hostile stack (21-day trial, auto-pause, one-tap cancel) is ethical but, by their own admission, turns the base to confetti if week-6 responder retention lands under ~30% — the payment case rests on a diffuse benefit that is hard to feel as a dollar or a saved appointment.
- wow: **5** — 'An app that sheds its skin before you tune it out' is conceptually clever and the telemetry-driven self-modification would impress a designer, but the ADHD user seeing it cold sees another reminder app with costumes — the demo moment shows a notification, not a rescue.
- Strongest point: It is the only entry whose primary claim is falsifiable by its own dashboard — day-42 responder retention versus a static-nudge control — and its deterministic trust layer (model writes the words, cron pulls the trigger, forgiveness enforced by schema) reflects the most sophisticated understanding in the field of what must never be delegated to a stochastic system.
- Fatal weakness: The load-bearing scientific bet is backwards: notification habituation in this population is substantially pre-attentive and channel-level — the swipe happens before the words register — so infinite content novelty attacks the wrong stage of the orienting response, and Molt's own risk list concedes that if week-3 telemetry shows identical latency decay for novel versus repeated nudges, the entire engine premise fails with no fallback product underneath.

### wildcard — total 61/80

- pain_severity: **9** — By spanning initiation, waiting mode, hyperfocus exit, and time blindness, Callie sits on the day's 3-5 daily transition failures simultaneously — the aggregate pain surface is the largest in the contest and each component cluster survived skeptic attack.
- evidence_strength: **8** — Citations are accurate and unusually self-critical (flags the JMIR figure as population-general, the AFFiNE habituation claim as vendor-only, the 68% hyperfocus stat's n=50) — but the thesis stitches four 0.6-0.75-confidence verdicts together, and the claim that they are 'one identical shape' is an inference the evidence base never tests.
- underserved: **7** — The four skeptic-conceded gaps genuinely do share the inbound-agent shape and no incumbent occupies it — but Callie overlaps T-Minus's exact territory on the strongest wedge, drifts toward the crowded generic 'proactive AI assistant' category at the edges, and each sub-wedge has named adjacent incumbents (Dubbii, Tiimo, BugMe, free OS alerts).
- ai_necessity: **9** — Same load-bearing case as T-Minus — real-time negotiation of a freeze state cannot be scripted — plus speech-to-structure that genuinely eliminates the setup burden that kills systems; without the LLM the calls are robocalls and the product is an alarm.
- retention: **7** — Inbound delivery, nightly-expiring schema (nothing can rot), and compounding personalization are all behaviorally correct — but 3-5 calls a day is a heavy interruption diet that spends the answer-reflex habituation budget roughly twice as fast as T-Minus's lean cadence, and the daily 7:30pm anchor call is itself a daily obligation that can decay.
- feasibility: **6** — Four distinct call types, calendar OAuth, a dead-man alarm chain, panic line, and SMS fallback in 30 days with 2-3 engineers is the heaviest month-one scope among the call-based entries; the $25 price gives honest margin ($8-12 COGS) but per-user minute budgets for heavy users are untested UX.
- willingness_to_pay: **7** — $25/mo framed as 1/20th of an hour of coaching is defensible and the billing ethics (auto-pause, cancel-by-saying-cancel, no annual until week-26 retention exists) are exemplary — but it is the highest price in the field asked of the most burned audience, on a product whose breadth makes the core benefit harder to name in one sentence.
- wow: **8** — 'The assistant who calls first' is a genuinely resonant inversion and the waiting-mode contract spoken aloud ('your 2pm is mine') is a chills moment — slightly blunted because the pitch is a relationship rather than a single sharp rescue, which demos less viscerally than T-Minus's razor.
- Strongest point: The portfolio structure is the smartest strategic hedge in the contest — waiting mode's unvalidated mechanism costs Callie a feature, not the company — and the observation that four skeptic-conceded gaps share one shape (an agent that initiates contact and carries context) is the single best synthesis of the evidence base any team produced.
- Fatal weakness: Breadth is a behavioral cost, not just a build cost: five daily inbound calls across four intervention types accelerates exactly the answer-reflex habituation and call-fatigue the modality bet depends on avoiding, while spreading a 30-day build across four loops means none of them — including the freeze-rescue negotiation that carries the thesis — gets the per-moment refinement that makes the difference between a caring voice and a telemarketer.

## Judge: business

Ranking: adhd-tax (68) > ignition (65) > temporal = wildcard (60) > evergreen (49). Recoup wins on investor fundamentals: it sits on the only 0.8-confidence cluster with objective registry evidence, its willingness-to-pay story is denominated in visible recovered dollars rather than felt relief, its trigger arrives in the physical mailbox independent of app engagement, and its unit economics don't depend on falling voice-API prices. T-Minus is the best product-thinking in the contest — the inbound-call inversion is the sharpest single idea — but it stacks answer-rate risk, carrier deliverability risk, and near-zero margin at $12/mo. Temporal and wildcard tie: Pacenote is more focused and cheaper to run but rests its flagship on the one mechanism the evidence base explicitly says is unvalidated while fighting free OS features; Callie correctly spots that all the skeptic-conceded gaps share one shape but is a seed-stage platform bet at the highest price with the broadest scope and two unvalidated mechanisms stacked. Molt is the most intellectually honest submission and the cheapest to build, but a seed check needs more than a falsifiable hypothesis — its lane is the most saturated, its wow is the weakest, and its core mechanic (content novelty beating channel-level habituation) is the field's least-supported bet. Notably, all five teams converged on auto-pause billing as trust fuel, so it differentiates no one; the theses were separated by where the retention trigger lives (physical world > inbound call > calendar event > notification) and whether week-6 value is legible in dollars.
